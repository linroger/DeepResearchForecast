# DeepResearchForecast from first principles, second pass (2026-10-27)

**What this is.** A design review, not a change. Inputs: the three ADRs, the reflexive world-state design, the two reviews of 2026-10-23 (`docs/handoff/pipeline-improvement-ideas/`), three code maps made for this review (the OASIS decision loop, world-v1, research v3 and the stage handoffs), and the newest live run as the specimen: the cybersecurity-industry question, `pipe_9d9b84efcd3d` → `sim_2a4f7d0e73de` → `report_38afe0543859`. Where the earlier reviews were right I say so briefly and move on; most of what follows is new, in particular the answer on the simulation–report relationship.

## 1. The one picture

The pipeline produces three estimates of the same four scenarios, and they never meet:

| Estimator | Where it comes from | Specimen |
|---|---|---|
| Research frame | the planner call, before any KIQ evidence: ≤6 scout searches and a 6,000-char digest; then enforced on every restatement by QA | 45 / 25 / 20 / 10 |
| Report "spine" | one LLM call in the report stage reading ≤2,000 chars of situation brief, ≤6,000 of forecast inputs, ≤6,000 of market pack and ≤5,000 of official data, told to add a "status quo/other" bucket | 38 / 15 / 20 / 7 + 20 other |
| Simulation shares | a head count of self-declared scenario votes (every actor's outcome power defaults to 1.0), stepped from the frame's weights, plus an ~8% "world channel" from the arbiter | 84.6 / 13.5 / 1.3 / 0.7, "did not stabilise" |

Two details make this worse than it looks. The frame's weights never reach the spine: the spine's input block renders a scenario probability only from a `probability_band` field that v3 never writes (`backend/app/utils/actors.py` ~2978), so the report's scenario probabilities are a fresh reading of digests, neither the research's own weighting nor an update of it (the weights do reach the binary extractor indirectly, because it reads the research prose that carries the canonical frame block). And the simulation does start from the frame, so the one estimator seeded by the research prior is the one forbidden from touching the probabilities, which is exactly the circularity ADR 0003 names.

None of the three computes the quantity the question asks about. The world model holds 32 variables; 25 ended the run at their seed value; the ones that moved were a 247.5→252.0 drift in a Gartner estimate, "consortium members 3→5.4875", and status strings the arbiter invented. Worldwide information-security spending in 2027, the quantity the binaries resolve on, is not a state variable, has no mechanism, and is moved by nobody.

The principle this review is built on: **a forecasting pipeline needs one explicit target, one structural model of what moves it, several estimators with different error structures, and a declared, measured rule for combining them.** Today there are three estimators with the same error structure (an LLM reading a digest), no structural model, and no combination rule beyond "the spine wins; print the others". Everything below follows from fixing that.

## 2. Deep research: from topic-led to target-led

### What it does now
v3 is a good report writer. Plan → 7 KIQs (one per outline section) → one tool-using agent each (3–5 searches, 3–6 fetches) → one gap round → write → QA → re-extract the typed artifacts from the finished prose by LLM. In the specimen: 201 ledger sources, 34 fetched (17%), none with a publication date, none with independence set; facebook.com (10) and linkedin.com (7) among the top domains; one gap round with three follow-ups. KIQs feed sections, sections feed a capped digest, the digest feeds the writer, and the writer's prose is then mined for `quantitative.json`, `actors.json` and the frame. The VERIFIED/REPORTED tags the gather phase earns do not survive that round trip; `sources.json` stores the first 1,200 characters of each page, not the passage that was cited.

The 2026-10-23 review's §2–3 (pivotal-claim register, evidence-conditioned revision, independence, freshness) are right; I will not repeat them. What I add is a change in the unit of organisation.

### Organise research around a resolution tree, not an outline
The question is "what will X be by date D". The natural decomposition is not twelve report sections but a **resolution tree**: the target quantity or event; the accounting or causal identities that determine it; the drivers of each term; the observable indicators of each driver; the settlement source and its release calendar; and the actors who hold a lever over each term. For the specimen: 2027 spending = 2026 base × (1 + g); g = trend (10–15% across five Gartner vintages) + incident-shock term + regulatory-mandate term + AI-securing-AI segment term; each term has a current reading, a vintage, a base rate, and two or three actors who can move it (CISA on CIRCIA, the Commission on AI Act milestones, Microsoft and CrowdStrike on pricing). KIQs become nodes of the tree with an explicit status (settled with value, vintage and source; contested; unknown). The outline is generated from the tree, not the tree from the outline.

This matters beyond tidiness: the tree is the one artifact research, simulation and report can share without translation. The simulation's state variables should *be* the tree's terms; the report's binaries should *be* predicates over its nodes; the spine's probabilities should be derived by propagating uncertainty through it, not by a fresh reading of a digest.

### Breadth and depth as budget allocation, not presets
- **Breadth** should come from independent retrieval strategies per node, not more searches per KIQ: publisher-first (the settlement source's own releases), data-first (the six official MCPs, already wired), primary-document (filings, rule texts, transcripts), adversarial (the strongest evidence against the leading scenario, mandatory as its own KIQ), and a second-language pass where the question warrants it. Tier the ledger so social platforms and aggregators cannot be the sole origin of a load-bearing number, and require at least one fetched primary page per pivotal claim; today a VERIFIED tag needs only the numbers to appear on *a* fetched page.
- **Depth** should follow a value-of-information triage computed over the tree: sensitivity of the target to the node × remaining uncertainty × likelihood that evidence exists ÷ cost. The gap reviewer today sees per-KIQ counts and three 120-char open questions; it cannot see which node matters. Give it the tree with sensitivities and let it spend follow-ups on single-origin pivotal claims, unresolved numeric conflicts (there is no deterministic adjudication of conflicting numbers today) and empty high-sensitivity nodes.
- **Stopping** by status (every pivotal node settled or declared unknowable), never by "fewer than three new VERIFIED facts".

### Make the assessment a versioned object with an update ledger
Freeze a prior (what the planner believed from the scout), then after gathering produce a posterior with a per-item ledger: evidence id → node → direction → size → new weight. A scenario-definition change is a version bump, never a silent update. The report prints the ledger. The simulation takes the structure and parameters (the tree), not the posterior weights (§5). Today there is no place where evidence changes a probability: QA rewrites any restated weight back to the plan-time value.

### Keep
The source ledger, canonical URLs, run-wide query dedup, passage selection and the prompt-cache layout are good engineering. The method skills (hazards KIQ, risk extraction with a stated-probability rule) are the right instinct; the specimen's single surviving risk row ("78% a state actor steals model weights, before 2030") shows how thin the supply of published hazards is, which the tree should carry as "unknown, no published base rate".

## 3. The simulation: what agents, what adjudicator, what for

### Diagnosis in principle
The two engines are opposite failure modes of the same missing thing.

**OASIS/reflexive is narration with bookkeeping.** Variables change only when the arbiter LLM says so; no action kind maps to any variable mechanically, and an option's `lag` delays nothing. The arbiter's values are then clamped to ±50% per step, pulled 15% back toward the research seed and held within ±35% of it, dampers it is never told about, so the world is an LLM's opinion mean-reverted to the research. Scenario shares are self-declared votes (116 of 136 in the specimen went to the leading scenario, and the share path compounded to 84.6%), weighted by an outcome power that is 1.0 for everyone because no dossier declares one. Relationships and reputation are rendered into briefs and consumed by nothing. Agents are configured as personas with a class catalogue: the cast was chosen because the names "matched an existing graph entity", 8 of 15 had no documented plans so their menus were class-generic, and several classes were wrong (CrowdStrike as `individual_leader`, Anthropic as `regulator_agency`, Munich Re as `research_institution`). Each period an actor picks "the ONE most consequential move" from ≤10 options; in practice 53 of 138 decisions were `fulfill:` (servicing commitments the mechanics created), 24 were "advance plan_1" (the same plan re-offered every round), 19 were public statements; there were 3 coercive and 4 policy moves in fourteen months of an industry under shock. Communication is public statements plus offers whose terms are free text; roughly three quarters of the 604 LLM calls were social-feed posts and reactions that re-enter decisions as twelve 240-char lines of "statements, not verified facts". The arbiter's validation is all-or-nothing and failed 4 of 14 rounds. It also reads the movers' private expected reactions, the one place where a private channel leaks into the world.

**world-v1 is a typed board game:** deterministic reducer, conservation, consent, actor-private observations, replayable commits. Those are the right primitives. But it has eight action kinds; mechanisms must be declared up front or the whole world fails as `modeling_gap`; nearly every parameter ends up a `scenario_assumption` because prose numbers cannot bind to typed parameters; messages go to template-fixed recipients; beliefs are free text replaced wholesale each decision; there is no outcome mapping to scenarios or forecast variables; the live behaviour gate was not met (16 of 18 contrasts); and it was evaluated on synthetic specs, so the compiler path never ran live.

Neither engine has the thing a strategic forecast simulation exists to provide: **a causal model of the target that actors act upon.** Without it, "reflexivity" is text about text.

### Design: mechanism first, agents second

**World = the resolution tree as a quantitative model.** State is the tree's terms (stocks, rates, policy settings) with uncertainty ranges and vintages from research. Mechanisms are the edges: identities (spending = base × growth), reaction functions (a regulator's rule-making cadence; pass-through of a tariff to a price), and elicited elasticities where no documented relation exists, stored as wide priors with a stated gap, not as hard failures (world-v1's mistake) and not as narrative (OASIS's). A small library of mechanism templates per domain (market-size growth, policy-rate reaction, trade-measure pass-through, supply–demand balance, adoption S-curve, regulatory milestone) covers most questions; the compiler binds research facts to template parameters where a typed quantity exists and elicits a bounded prior where it does not, recording which is which. Fewer variables, each load-bearing: the specimen's 32 incidental facts should have been perhaps eight terms.

**Actors = holders of levers.** Select the cast by lever ownership and exposure over the tree, not graph centrality: who can set a term (decision right), who is paid or hurt by it (stake), who knows something about it (private signal). Configure each as an institution: objectives as preferences over tree variables, hard constraints (mandate, budget, legal process, political cost), levers with ranges, lags and reversibility, an information set, and beliefs as a private forecast of the key terms. Decision rules of mixed fidelity: rule-based for actors well approximated by a reaction function (central banks, statutory calendars, mechanical hedgers), LLM for genuinely discretionary strategic actors, with the LLM-decided roster small (five to eight). That alone removes most of the 3.3M tokens and most of the cheap talk.

**Adjudicator = four separated jobs, not one narrator.**
1. A deterministic *mechanism engine* that propagates lever settings and shocks through the tree each period: the physics that does not exist today.
2. A typed *effect estimator*, called once per unknown edge at compile time (and when a new, unmodelled interaction appears), returning a bounded parameter prior with a basis; never a per-round opinion about every variable.
3. A *feasibility judge* for custom or ambiguous moves: does the actor hold the right, the capacity, the counterparty's consent; its output is accept or reject with a reason and, if accepted, the typed lever edit. It never invents variables and never sees anyone's private rationale or expectations.
4. The *exogenous sampler* (shocks from documented hazards, already built) plus a *consistency reviewer* that samples the estimator twice and widens the range on disagreement instead of picking one.

Validation becomes repair-by-field, not reject-the-round; a failed field falls back to the mechanism's own propagation.

**Action grammar.** Actions are lever settings with magnitude and timing (tariff to 25% effective Q2; capex +$2bn over 18 months; rule finalised with 90-day compliance), commitments (a typed future lever setting with a due date and a condition), offers whose terms are typed commitments by each party, coalition acts that create joint commitments with an enforcement clause, and signals (announce, threaten, promise, deny) that carry a typed claim about a lever or a variable. One move per period is wrong for most institutions: an actor should hold a plan (a sequence of intended lever settings) with triggers ("if the incident count crosses N, accelerate"), re-decided only when a trigger fires, on cadence, or when a counterparty acts on it. That is cheaper and more realistic than re-electing one move from a menu every month.

**Influence between actors, mechanically.** Signals update other actors' beliefs through an expectation channel weighted by the speaker's credibility, a track record that is actually used (acceptance probability of offers, weight of a threat in the target's belief update), not just rendered. Consent governs anything bilateral (world-v1's rule, already partly ported). Coalitions pool levers and share enforcement. Red lines are predicates over variables, so a crossing is detected by the engine, not by the arbiter's reading. Expectations move variables where the mechanism says they do (markets, procurement intentions): that is the real reflexive loop, belief → announced lever → counterparties' beliefs → their levers → variables → beliefs.

**Information.** Each actor sees the tree's variables through an observation model (public series on their release schedule, with lag; private signals for own-firm quantities; noisy estimates of rivals), holds a private forecast, and is scored on it; the "actor foresight Brier" the earlier review proposed becomes natural once beliefs are typed. The brief is rendered per actor from relevance to its levers and stakes, not one 3,200-char brief for everyone.

**Discourse.** Keep OASIS, if at all, as a narrative layer for the record, not as decision input. Replace its role in the loop with one or two aggregate state variables (public salience, market sentiment) updated by a cheap mechanism or a handful of structured audience samples. Twelve lines of unverified statements are not how institutions read the world.

**Uncertainty and output.** Run K closed-loop paths that draw the elicited parameters and shocks consistently per path and re-elicit decisions only when triggers differ between paths (the earlier review's §6, made affordable by triggers). The simulation's native output is then a distribution over the target variable per path; scenario probabilities fall out by evaluating scenario predicates (each scenario defined as a region of outcome space, which the frame should have been all along): no votes, no pressure words, no entropy floor. That output is comparable with the research posterior as a second estimator with a different error structure, which is the only reason to want a second estimator.

### Cost, honestly
Compiling mechanisms from prose is the hard part; world-v1 proved it by turning everything into assumptions. The mitigations are the template library, binding parameters to typed research quantities where the tree already has them, gaps as wide priors rather than failures, and a small variable set. The realism bar is the earlier review's: a well-observed domain first (capacity deployment, a regulatory process, a commodity balance), replay and placebo arms before any claim.

## 4. Context: one case file, projections instead of re-extraction

The context-propagation audit fixed real plumbing bugs (CTX-L1–L9) but, by its own scope, did not address semantic loss, and the maps show the pattern: typed → prose → LLM → typed, repeatedly.

- Research facts are typed and tagged per KIQ, flattened into a capped digest (lowest-priority lines dropped first: open questions, UNVERIFIED, REPORTED, conflicts, then VERIFIED), written as prose, after which `actors.json`, `quantitative.json`, `contested.json` and the frame are re-extracted from that prose by three LLM calls. Tags, KIQ provenance and the cited passage are lost; sources carry no date.
- The research report is chunked (2,500 chars) and re-extracted again into the graph by Graphiti's LLM; the report writer reaches research content mainly through graph tools, since the research text is not pinned into section prompts.
- PREPARE's context is 50,000 chars with a 4,000-char actors digest that keeps only the first goal per actor; the forecast-inputs block (8,000) carries no risks and no probabilities; v3 exports no horizon, so the horizon is re-derived by two LLM calls; actor plans and red lines are inferred from goals and constraints by heuristics.
- In the loop: public brief 3,200 chars, private 1,300 (red lines rendered last, so first to be cut), discourse 12 × 240, arbiter ≤24 variables with four-value histories, statements truncated at 4,000 / 800 / 320 / 240 / 160 depending on which consumer reads them.
- The spine sees ≤2,000 chars of situation brief and never the frame's weights; binaries are extracted from 48,000 chars of research prose, head and tail.

The fix is structural, not more characters. **One typed case file per run** (question and resolution contract, resolution tree with node status, pivotal-claim register with sids and passages, actor/lever registry, mechanism graph with parameter bases, assessment versions with the update ledger, simulation paths and discoveries, report) as the single authority, which is what ADR 0002 already says of the evidence ledger and the bundle. Every stage reads and writes typed nodes; **prose is rendered from the case file and never parsed back.** Concretely: ontology and graph seeded deterministically from the tree and registry, with LLM extraction only on uncovered text; PREPARE compiled from the registry, not from a 50k-char head of the report; actor briefs rendered per actor by relevance; the spine computed over the tree and then explained by an LLM rather than guessed by one; the report's numbers rendered from typed rows with their sids, so the number-provenance check becomes a join rather than a regex. Carry `sid`, `node_id`, `claim_id` and `variable_id` end to end, so a report sentence can cite the research fact and a simulation variable can cite the node it instantiates.

## 5. What the relationship between the simulation and the report should be

The question has a sharper form once §1 is in view: the simulation is already downstream of the research frame (it starts from its weights) and already excluded from the report's numbers (ADR 0003). So "reverse the order" is not quite the choice. The real choice is **what the simulation is conditioned on, and what it is allowed to produce.** Three positions:

**A. Independent and parallel (today's letter, if not its spirit).** Research → {report, simulation}, with the simulation's output shown beside the report's probabilities in one fenced section. *Pros:* the isolation is provable (byte-identical prompts), laundering is closed, nothing speculative can be mistaken for evidence. *Cons:* the simulation's share path has no meaning (it is a vote count), so the table shows an 84.6-versus-38 divergence nobody can interpret; 3.3M tokens produce an appendix; the stage reads as decorative, which is the complaint ADR 0003 itself records.

**B. Report first, then a simulation constrained to the report ("mimic").** *Pros:* the simulation gets the best-informed parameters, and it can act as a consistency check: does any coherent sequence of actor decisions and mechanisms actually produce the report's modal path at the report's numbers, and what has to be true along the way? That is a genuine use, a stress test of the narrative. *Cons:* if the report's probabilities are the constraint, the simulation can only confirm them; it inherits the report's blind spots exactly; every "discovery" is circular; and it anchors the arbiter and the actors, who will steer toward the stated central view. Your suspicion is correct: a simulation of the report is a dramatisation, not an estimator.

**C. Shared inputs, independent conclusions, declared combination (my recommendation).** Condition the simulation on the research's structure and evidence (the resolution tree, parameter ranges, actor levers and stakes, documented hazards) and never on the research's or the report's probabilities: no frame weights as the share prior, no spine in any prompt, no scenario names as votes. Let it produce its own distribution over the target by mechanism plus decisions. Then the report combines the two estimators under a declared, versioned rule, with weight zero and shadow-only until a prospective paired lift exists (I-21, as ADR 0003 requires), promotable only through the outcome-blind gate the earlier review proposed. Whatever the weight, the report explains the divergence: which assumptions, actors or mechanisms the simulation needed to reach what the research posterior believes, and which paths reach the tails.

Under C the simulation has five uses, none of which requires it to move a probability:
1. **Mechanism consistency.** Does the research's modal scenario have a feasible path? The specimen's research says 45% trend acceleration; a mechanism model of spending growth, regulatory calendars and vendor pricing would say whether that is reachable without an incident, and the gap is informative either way.
2. **Tail discovery.** Interaction paths to extremes that reference-class research structurally misses: escalation spirals, coordination failures, cascades through commitments.
3. **Sensitivity.** Which parameters and which actors' decisions move the target distribution most. That output should drive the next research round's budget (the bounded investigation loop of the earlier review's §7).
4. **Signposts.** Branch points in the paths become dated, observable indicators for the report's watchlist and for the resolution monitor.
5. **A second estimator with a different error structure**, measured in shadow against outcomes until it earns a weight.

On order: produce the research posterior (typed, versioned) before the simulation and freeze it, which gives the isolation test and an honest divergence, but write the prose report last, from the case file, with both estimators in it. Today's sequence (simulation → report) is fine in that sense; what is wrong is the simulation's inputs (the frame prior, thin and misclassified actors, no mechanism) and its output (a head count). Reversing the order would fix neither and would add the anchoring of B.

What remains of independence under C is the thing that matters: **independence of errors.** Two estimators that read the same evidence but reason by different machinery (reference classes and evidence weighting versus mechanisms and strategic interaction) can disagree informatively. Two estimators that share a prior, or where one constrains the other, cannot.

## 6. Worth fixing now regardless (from the code maps)
- The frame's weights never reach the spine (`probability` versus `probability_band`); decide deliberately whether the spine should see them and end the accidental independence.
- Remove scenario votes from the share path, or at least weight them by resolution authority and by the arbiter's resolution of the move; today a blocked move votes as much as a successful one.
- Repair arbiter output by field instead of rejecting the round (4 of 14 rounds lost).
- Stop passing private `expected_reactions` to the arbiter.
- Make capacity bind: it resets each round with one move per round, custom moves are unchecked, and several catalogue entries can never appear because their cost exceeds the class capacity.
- Agreements due from their own terms with a grace period; `fulfill:` should not consume the actor's single move.
- Cast selection and agency class from documented decision rights, with a stated basis; refuse to seed a cast where most actors have no plans.
- Date every fetched page and search row; fill `independent` by origin clustering; demote social platforms as sole origins.
- The decision call reuses the OASIS "perform actions by tool calling" system template while demanding JSON; temperature is inert under `claude-cli`, so the K-path sibling temperatures do nothing.

## 7. Sequencing
1. **Resolution tree and settlement contract from research** (a new phase after plan: nodes with status, sensitivity and settlement source). Acceptance: the specimen's tree names 2027 spending as the target with five terms, each with a current value, vintage and source; the binaries are predicates over nodes.
2. **Simulation state = tree terms; shares derived from the target distribution via scenario predicates; votes removed.** Acceptance: a run with no actor decisions reproduces the tree's own baseline path; a run with decisions moves only through declared levers.
3. **Lever-based actor registry with mixed-fidelity decision rules and triggers; adjudicator split.** Acceptance: capacity and rights bind; a blocked move changes nothing; credibility changes an offer's acceptance probability.
4. **Case file as authority with projections** (ontology and graph seeded deterministically; briefs rendered per actor; spine computed, then explained). Acceptance: report numbers byte-identical to typed rows; no LLM re-extraction of research prose.
5. **Closed-loop K paths with per-path parameter draws; sensitivity and signposts into the report; shadow combination rule.** Acceptance: a paired, outcome-blind comparison enrolled prospectively, on the forward cohort the earlier review specifies.

Measurement first and throughout, as the 2026-10-23 inventory insists: nothing above should be believed until outcomes resolve against it.
