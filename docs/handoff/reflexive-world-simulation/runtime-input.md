## 1. Decision recommended for the plan

Introduce an explicitly selected, versioned `world_simulation` runtime with **one child-owned round controller and one authoritative world commit per round**. Put deterministic feasibility/resolution logic behind a pure kernel boundary; keep actor proposal generation, persistence, platform presentation and process control outside it. Preserve legacy OASIS runs under their current engine identity. The existing scalar scenario-share model should remain an explicitly labeled legacy diagnostic, not become the storage model for concrete resources, rights, commitments and relationships.

Keep the present parent/child process topology. A separate world-engine entry path is cleaner than inserting another side effect into the two independently advancing legacy loops. A small child entry script is a reasonable implementation choice, subject to coordinator approval; it entails extending dispatch, process-identity recognition and completion gates listed below. It does not require a new service, broker or distributed coordinator. If OASIS participates in world mode, adapt it to a bounded round operation driven by the controller; calling either existing whole-run coroutine is insufficient.

## 2. Existing implementation and concrete causal limits

1. **WorldState is a normalized scenario distribution.** It aggregates `magnitude × weight`, blends toward the resulting distribution using inertia, and optionally mixes back toward the seed prior. It has no resource inventory, ownership transfer, action prerequisite, counterpart acceptance, pending commitment or delayed-effect queue. `outcome()` explicitly calls the result an `elicited_model_projection`. Unknown outcome power is separately marked and defaults to neutral 1.0; it is not an enforceable budget. Retain the distinction between visibility and authority, but do not reuse the weight as physical or institutional capacity. [W1] [W2] [D2]

2. **The decision channel infers scenario commitments from a roster; it does not execute feasible actor actions.** One batched model call receives stance, gains/losses, optional short post text and immutable scenario priors, and returns `{agent_id, scenario, magnitude, confidence}`. Its shown parser filters scenario names but does not enforce exactly one decision per expected actor, roster membership, or resource feasibility. It declares abstention if no commitment remains and any abstention was encountered, rather than proving every expected actor answered. Tail actors can collapse into a weighted public block. Those are unsuitable contracts for individual asset ownership or voting rights. [D1] [D2]

3. **Existing feedback is principally text plus scenario momentum.** In-band evolution builds the decision roster from actors found in actual action rows, deduplicates by `agent_id`, and takes the first available post. Actors making consequential private decisions without posting need a separate participation rule. The calendar clock tells actors to treat their post as their consequential action; the executable platform action unions are social operations. The delta formatter takes scheduled text, up to five posts ranked by influence and a direction-only momentum line, capped at 900 characters. This does not establish that an announced investment, alliance or policy actually happened. [L1] [L2] [E2] [W3]

4. **Watermarks order evolution, but do not create a causal barrier.** `deliver()` and `heartbeat()` are synchronous. `_advance()` waits until platform watermarks permit processing; neither caller waits for a shared round result before starting its next period. Each loop stores `latest_delta()` in a local string at its own round end. A fast platform can start round r+1 before the other submits r; even after the slower platform resolves r, the faster platform's stored string is not automatically refreshed. Whole-run `asyncio.gather(return_exceptions=True)` isolates platforms but does not synchronize their rounds. [E1] [L3] [L4] [P2]

5. **Empty participation, failed execution and missing world accounting can collapse together.** Both no-active-actor and `env.step` failure branches heartbeat, log zero actions and advance their platform checkpoint. Heartbeats create no pending-round buffer. If both platforms heartbeat a round, it need not produce any world trajectory entry; if only one platform fails and the other delivers actions, the merged elicitation has no explicit failed-platform marker. `_advance(flush_all=True)` can process outstanding partial deliveries after a platform exception. WorldState already freezes shares and convergence EWMA for invalid decision statuses, and convergence requires minimum valid coverage and zero failed rounds. Those guards are valuable, but cover elicitation status, not complete platform participation. A real barrier must carry explicit participation/status receipts and must not infer success from a watermark. [E1] [E2] [L3] [L4] [W1] [W2]

6. **Scheduled announcements are not committed transitions.** `fire_scheduled_events()` logs the manual CREATE_POST before awaiting the environment step; an environment exception returns zero. `_scheduled_events_due()` independently supplies all configured due rows to the clock and evolution. Consequently, the actor-visible “confirmed” event list is not a receipt of successful execution. Duplicate announcements are deduplicated by `(date, content)` in the evolution buffer, not by a durable causal event ID. Reuse date/period navigation; give exogenous changes and delayed completions separate durable identities and effects. [L2] [E1] [L3]

7. **Post-hoc replay cannot supply missing reflexive execution.** It precomputes unique roster/date elicitation tasks concurrently, then steps shares in order. The runtime log reader passes only round and actor identity/name. It cannot reconstruct actor choices conditioned on concrete intermediate worlds. Its run-level `validity` and `forecast_effect` labeling is useful precedent: invalid/inconclusive runs get `no_update`; valid ones remain at most diagnostic. In-band finalization has a different outer result shape, though its nested WorldState outcome carries epistemic and round accounting fields. Do not assume the post-hoc outer validity fields are present on every existing trajectory. [D3] [D4] [E2] [P2]

## 4. Minimal runtime contract recommendations

These are responsibilities and invariants for the coordinator to turn into final interfaces, not competing API definitions.

**Pure kernel.** Accept a versioned prior world, period, applicable due events, a complete decision batch, explicit mechanism parameters and recorded randomness. Return validation outcomes, accepted/rejected effects, pending commitments, next world and observation-relevant events. The kernel performs no LLM calls, platform writes or filesystem operations. It checks finite quantities/units, actor identity, authorization, prerequisite dependencies, resource conservation and joint conflicts. An unknown action or unavailable causal rule yields an explicit unsupported/infeasible outcome; it does not authorize narrative world editing.

**Actor adapter.** Receive only the actor's admitted profile, goals/incentives/constraints, durable private belief/memory state and permitted observation. Return a structured intent or explicit abstention and a proposed belief/memory update. Bind the response to actor, round, attempt and observation hash. Validate one response per expected actor and deduplicate multiple platform handles against the canonical actor mapping. Visibility and public speech do not confer control over assets or votes. Persist the observations actually used; do not rely on reconstructing arbitrary CAMEL conversation memory after restart.

**Round controller.** Use one authoritative period sequence and a frozen required-participant set for each round. Recommended sequence:

1. Read committed state S(r−1). Stage due completions and exogenous events at declared period boundaries; derive a common working snapshot and actor-specific observations. Persist the tentative observation inputs and any draws used to produce them before requesting decisions, so retries use the same information. Nothing is published as committed yet. Events due later inside a coarse period must wait for the defined end boundary or an explicit subperiod; their future outcome must not leak into start-of-period knowledge.
2. Collect bounded concurrent actor responses for round r. Every selected actor has a terminal response status; intentionally inactive actors have an explicit participation record. No actor may begin r+1 while r remains unresolved. All required adapter initialization failures, timeouts and cancellations release waiting tasks through an explicit failed attempt, never a success heartbeat.
3. Validate the whole batch and resolve conflicts against one snapshot under the selected domain rules. Reserve resources for accepted delayed commitments atomically with other effects. Arrival order must not decide allocation; use declared priorities/tie-breaking, with any randomness recorded. A rejected but well-formed decision is a valid completed response, distinct from provider failure.
4. Commit the next world, events, pending queue and durable actor-state updates together. Only then release the next round and publish progress/observation material derived from that commit. Counterparty/rival responses normally occur next round; optional within-period negotiation needs an explicit bounded phase and its own barrier, not an unconstrained reaction loop.

**Decision failure policy for the first version:** a required actor's missing, malformed or failed decision prevents committing r and prevents advancing simulated time. Preserve the prior committed world and record the failed attempt; retry only missing/failed responses within a bounded policy, reusing already durable responses against the same observation hashes. After the retry limit, return a resumable failed execution status. Explicit abstention still permits due events and valid peers' actions to progress. This policy distinguishes a computational outage from an actor deliberately doing nothing, and avoids importing the current “failed step → advance checkpoint” behavior. Degraded partial-round progression, if later requested, requires its own pinned semantics and acceptance evidence.

**Domain mechanisms.** Share scheduling, identity, visibility, batching, validation plumbing and commits across domains. Put causal formulas, legal powers, quantity units, settlement/cancellation rules and decision thresholds in versioned bounded mechanisms. Synthetic investment/supply-chain can use explicit budget debits and delayed capacity delivery; policy/trade can use enumerated enactment authority and effective-date route restrictions; coalition/institutional can use membership, voting eligibility, quorum and ratification rules. Do not infer a universal price, welfare, trust, trade elasticity or belief-update equation. Mechanism parameters must retain evidence/assumption/unknown status. Repeated model scenario frequencies remain conditional model outputs, not calibrated forecast probabilities.

## 5. Durability, replay and old-run compatibility

**Observed legacy recovery:** platform checkpoints hold completed round, trace cursor, action count, config hash and a snapshot of the shared process RNG. Writes are best-effort and can silently fail. Parent resume selection takes the maximum valid platform round; the child restores each separately. It keeps the DB, advances its trace cursor to at least the current DB maximum, and skips seeds. World evolution is recreated from its seed; actor conversation memory is not restored by these checkpoint fields. The comments explicitly document partial-round loss and clock/memory reset. These are partial-state continuation semantics, not a world transaction. [C1] [C2] [R4]

**Failure windows to close:** platform DB writes can precede extraction or checkpointing; a failed step may have partially changed platform state without a matching accepted decision receipt. World shares/history mutate before digest append, and trajectory/decisions are written later at finalization. A digest write failure is caught after mutation. Per-file atomic rename does not join these writes into a cross-file commit. One shared RNG with independent platform checkpoints also does not define a coherent global recovery point. These are source-derived hazards; no live failure was injected in this assignment. [L3] [E2] [C1] [C3]

**Recommended bounded persistence choice:** a single-writer transactional world ledger, separate from OASIS DBs, is a practical starting point. Keep one durable round commit containing parent/next state hashes, full checkpoint state, decisions/statuses, event identities, pending commitments, durable actor memory/beliefs, mechanism/config bindings and random draws/state. Durable proposal receipts may precede the commit but must be labeled tentative and tied to a specific attempt and observation. The commit and authoritative head advance must be atomic. Derived JSON summaries, UI progress and optional social announcements are rebuildable projections, identified by run generation and commit; publication retries deduplicate by commit/event ID.

Recovery loads the last complete committed world, validates its engine/config/mechanism closure, and reuses recorded proposal receipts where appropriate. It never silently reseeds world mode from a legacy platform checkpoint. A crash before commit discards tentative effects; a crash after commit re-exports projections without reapplying effects. Record every nondeterministic input required for reducer replay. Exact effect replay from stored decisions is achievable without asserting repeatability of a fresh model call. A model response lost before its receipt is persisted can require a new call; no external-call exactly-once guarantee is claimed.

If mutable OASIS feeds are inputs to future world decisions, they also need recovery from the same committed generation or deterministic reconstruction. Keeping a partially mutated DB and skipping its trace tail is not acceptable for that mode. The smallest first slice can keep social rendering downstream of committed events and keep causal observations in the world ledger; bidirectional social coupling is a separate bounded acceptance concern.

Compatibility rules for the plan:

- Missing engine metadata follows the existing legacy read/run path. Existing trajectory schemas and their epistemic labels remain distinguishable; do not relabel historical social simulations as concrete-world runs.
- Explicit world mode requires its new schema, input closure and commit chain. Missing/corrupt/mismatched world evidence fails closed before any file rotation, DB deletion or fallback execution. Unknown engine versions are not treated as legacy.
- Continue a historical run only under its original engine's capabilities. To explore a concrete world from historical research, create a new prepared generation with declared initial-state assumptions; do not manufacture inventories or commitments from scenario shares.
- New run generations isolate world artifacts from earlier attempts, including failure before the first commit. Completion checks, summary generation and reuse must inspect the same generation and engine. A retained interview child can outlive completion without modifying the committed world.
- Do not terminate because scenario shares converge. Stop at the declared horizon or an explicit stop/failure; expose commitments still pending beyond the horizon and incomplete coverage honestly.

## 7. Coordinator decisions and explicit uncertainties

- Finalize the engine selector, actor identity mapping, observation/action/event types and cross-stage manifests centrally. This note recommends responsibilities, not parallel definitions.
- Choose and freeze the ledger format, failure/attempt lifecycle and domain conflict policies before implementation. Transactional SQLite is a recommendation, not an existing world store or a proven power-loss guarantee; durability settings and commit/export recovery require A6 evidence.
- Bound OASIS's role in the first approved slice. If it influences future observations, specify platform-state recovery and effective prompt capture; if it only displays committed events, it can remain outside causal authority. Source inspection cannot prove third-party `env.step` transactionality or effective prompt retention.
- The actor/research owner must define provenance for initial resources, institutional rights and private state. The consumer owner must define world-mode summaries and simulated graph separation. Current source maps are navigation, not proof these new contracts already exist.
- The inspected callsites support the seams above. No installed provider behavior, saved-run correctness, live-world fidelity, throughput or forecast accuracy was evaluated. All eight scenarios remain planned until the reviewed PLANS.md receives user approval and implementation begins.
