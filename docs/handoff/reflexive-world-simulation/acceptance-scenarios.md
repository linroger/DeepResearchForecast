# Reflexive world simulation acceptance scenarios

**Status:** Proposed acceptance specification; no implementation or tests have run.
**Authority:** [PLANS.md](PLANS.md) and [contracts.md](contracts.md).

All values below are synthetic fixture values, not claims about a real company, state or institution. The three packs use the same actor/world/action/commit/result interfaces. M0 fixes their JSON fixtures, periods, exact expected event traces, accounting rules and hashes before implementation. Test doubles replace model/provider/graph boundaries; production compilers, controllers, stores, service/API and report consumers remain real.

## 1. Investment and supply-chain pack

Four principals are a producer, buyer, financier and permitting institution. Initial producer investment budget is 10 fixture currency units, current capacity is 4 units/period, and one declared project costs 6 units and adds 3 capacity after two full periods, subject to its permit. Capacity also depends on an explicit input/utility limit; installed capacity is not guaranteed production. External construction expenditure has a named accounting sink/counterparty.

The producer obtains authorization, commits the project and reserves/pays the declared amount once. A buyer sees a delivery shortfall and requests a supply contract; the supplier/financier decide their own consent. Capacity remains 4 before the defined completion boundary and becomes 7 exactly once afterward, subject to the input limit. The buyer's next observation/options and scripted order decision change when that completion is visible. The timeline links decision → accepted action → pending project → completion → later response.

Paired controls: budget 5 blocks the 6-unit investment; a mere expansion announcement does not add capacity; a longer delay preserves the shortage; a low input limit prevents installed capacity from becoming fictitious output; a hidden supplier condition does not change the buyer's visible menu until an authorized response/disclosure.

## 2. Policy and trade pack

Four principals are a rule-making institution, importer and two suppliers. Route A is initially eligible and has a lower declared delivery cost than route B. The institution can authorize a scoped restriction effective at the opening of period 3. Its mechanism defines authority, implementation lag, eligible transactions and treatment of already accepted orders explicitly; the fixture exempts already accepted orders rather than silently cancelling them.

A commentator or unauthorized actor cannot enact the restriction by announcement. Once the authorized rule becomes effective, new route-A requests become ineligible under the mechanism. The importer observes the public rule and chooses an allowed route-B order or an explicit infeasibility/wait response. Both suppliers retain their own capacity, budget and acceptance choices. No tariff-to-inflation/price elasticity is assumed unless the fixture declares one.

Paired controls: remove authority; move the effective date past the horizon; change the alternative route's capacity; delay disclosure; keep the physical world fixed but change only a nonbinding public statement. Expected effects and actor-visible responses must follow the actual rule and information boundary.

## 3. Coalition and institutional pack

Four principals are three fictional organizations with voting entitlements 45/30/25 and an executing institution. A motion requires 51 votes and eligible mandates. The initial resource envelope is 80 units; the proposed program costs 60. Organization A cannot pass it alone, bind B's vote or transfer B's resources. B has a distinct objective and can reject or condition support.

A offers exact coalition terms, B accepts or counters through the bounded response protocol, and formal votes determine the motion. Public promises are not votes. A passed motion becomes an executable pending obligation only if the institutional and resource requirements are satisfied. A later disclosed funding reduction or withdrawal changes feasible policy/coalition choices in the next period. Any contract breach is a distinct action with the fixture's declared consequences; values, beliefs and actual voting rights remain separate.

Paired controls: withhold B's consent; invalidate a mandate; lower available funds below 60; hide then reveal a partner's changed preference; permute response arrival order. Passage, resource use, relationship changes and later actor responses must match the declared institutional rules.

Across each pack, execute five paired interventions: binding resource/authority constraint, delayed consequence, hidden/revealed information, material counterparty response, and semantically irrelevant ordering/label changes. Scripted expected transitions must be declared before patches; model-driven label sensitivity is measured separately rather than falsely guaranteed away.


**Timing trace for RW-04/RW-08:** at time 0 the opening budget is 10. A newly accepted 6-unit project debits the post-decision phase, leaving 4 before any interval flows; it never rewrites the opening observation. A two-full-period completion occurs at time 2 before that boundary's next decision view. Add a half-period completion and a rule effective exactly at a boundary to check chronological flow accounting. At the terminal horizon, due work settles without an extra decision call. Freeze the phase/event sequence and replay it across a crash.

**Separate responsiveness gate:** each domain preregisters which material consequence-sensitive paired cases require a different next decision, its direction, and the comparison epoch. Passing those contrasts is required in addition to the aggregate 80% behavior score; explanatory prose or feasibility cannot compensate for no response. Missing or budget-stopped cases remain incomplete in the denominator. An ablation pair with no relevant difference supplies no evidence of feedback benefit.
## 5. Gate interpretation

Mechanical gates require zero hard physical/authority/visibility violations, zero duplicate settlements and complete references from consequential state changes to accepted events/mechanisms. A well-formed but rejected proposal is not a provider error. Unknown is not zero; inactive is not failed; horizon completion is not full settlement; modeler knowledge is not actor knowledge.

Evidence includes exact source/test hashes; fixture/model/assumption versions; emitted actor-visible bytes; decision and event traces; committed state hashes; real API payloads; rendered UI/report data; and fault/recovery outcomes. Existing guarded-runner receipts must show collection/setup success, controlled attempted operations and source stability. Fixture truth never substitutes for actual consumer execution.

Model behavior gets a separate preregistered held-out review against an external-feedback-ablation baseline. Score goal/incentive consistency, feasible choices, response to consequences and declared-explanation/record agreement on 0–2 scales, with at least 80% per-domain points and no hard validity/privacy violation as the proposed promotion threshold. Report invalid-action, abstention, retry, latency and token/call distributions per domain. No pass/fail result exists at planning time.

The proposed live matrix is 18 reflexive cases (3 domains × 3 held-out variants × 2 seeds) plus 18 matched external-feedback-ablation controls. With four actors and four periods, the actor-policy ceiling is 576 initial decision slots plus at most 576 recipient response slots. At two actual provider attempts maximum per slot, including all fallback/repair/retry, the worst-case actor-call cap is 2,304; the approved token/cost cap can stop the batch earlier. Strict fixture preparation is reused, while any separate model-compiler evaluation needs its own stated call allowance. This evaluation is launched only after an explicit total cost/token ceiling is approved. It tests behavior under declared mechanisms, not real-world probability calibration. Mechanical tests and scripted sensitivity runs execute offline first.

## 6. Definition of implementation done

All mandatory offline scenarios and existing affected regressions pass on reviewed source; both research input grades and all three packs reach actual world/API/UI/report consumers; old runs remain readable; run/round recovery and forecast isolation pass negative controls; documentation and bilingual UI reflect implemented behavior; source and review receipts match delivered code. M5's live behavior gate is separately evidenced before default admission or a claim of model-driven realism. The feature remains incomplete if only a pure reducer, a prose demonstration or a social-feed chart exists.

**Control semantics:** the external-feedback-ablation control keeps the same real transition kernel, resource/authority checks, exogenous schedule, own-account state and mandatory offer/consent/action-receipt protocol. It withholds a preregistered subset of external-world observation updates (for example a rival's publicly completed capacity expansion) from actor policies. The exact channel/field mask is part of each paired case; it cannot remove terms needed for a valid consent response or leak the withheld update through menus, summaries or retries. Counterfactual actors may still learn from subsequent permitted interaction outcomes; the measured contrast is the response at the predeclared decision epoch. Invalid attempts remain rejected by the kernel. This is a named evaluation intervention, not a production mode or a world in which conservation stops applying.
