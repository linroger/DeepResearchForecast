## 5. Regulatory Crossroads: Reserve Bills, CLARITY, GENIUS and SEC Rulemaking

Regulation in this cycle is not a single switch but four parallel tracks, each with its own clock, its own institutional sponsor and its own transmission channel into Bitcoin's price. The regulatory complex as it stands supports the range-bound digestion base case: it removes tail legal risk (a de-banking or outright prohibition outcome is effectively off the table) without yet supplying the fiscal or legitimating catalyst that the institutional reflation scenario needs.

### The Strategic Bitcoin Reserve: Symbolic by Construction

The Strategic Bitcoin Reserve, established by executive order on March 6, 2025, is best understood as a policy signal engineered to carry zero fiscal cost. [S15] It capitalized a reserve with the government's existing holdings, bars sales, and permits acquisition only through budget-neutral strategies. No open-market purchase by the US government has been documented. The executive order's constraint is structural, not rhetorical: new purchases require Congress.

That is why the legislative branch matters. The BITCOIN Act — the Begich-led legislation to establish a statutory Strategic Bitcoin Reserve with a large-scale federal acquisition program [S44] — would give the reserve actual purchase power; it has been introduced and reintroduced across sessions without becoming law. The American Reserve Modernization Act (ARMA) of 2026 would codify the reserve in the Treasury without taxpayer cost, with sales permitted only to reduce national debt; [S44] it has advanced no further than introduction.

No documented budget-neutral acquisition transaction has followed the executive order.

A steelman of the opposite view deserves weight: a sovereign accumulation signal, if it ever came, would be the single most reflexive bullish catalyst available — more durable than ETF flows because it is price-insensitive and publicly committed. But the observable evidence — no purchase power, no purchase, contested attribution — means the reserve currently functions as a floor-narrative rather than a bid.

### CLARITY: One Vote Short and What a Lame-Duck Revival Would Actually Deliver

The reconsideration motion keeps the bill alive into the lame-duck session, which is the only realistic window for enactment before the 119th Congress expires;

Two features of the failure matter for the forecast. First, it was a one-vote margin — not a repudiation. DLA Piper's September analysis and Orrick's October follow-up both frame the outcome as procedural gridlock rather than substantive rejection. Second, the SEC's own posture reduces the stakes of delay: Chairman Atkins has stated the SEC supports delivering the CLARITY Act and is acting via rulemaking in the meantime. The agency is substituting administrative action for stalled legislation — an explicit, documented strategy that changes what non-enactment means for markets. If a friendly SEC can deliver most of the practical clarity through rules, the legislative premium attached to CLARITY passage shrinks.

The lame-duck window supports the upside of that estimate (a single vote flip suffices); For BTC specifically, enactment operates through a legitimacy channel rather than direct demand: institutional compliance departments get a clear SEC/CFTC jurisdictional map, which at the margin eases allocations from fiduciaries.

### SEC Rulemaking: The Operative Channel, With a 2027 Clock

With CLARITY stalled, the SEC is where crypto policy is actually being made. Two proposals dominate:

- The proposal asserts SEC oversight of crypto assets including Bitcoin.

The custody proposal is the one with the most direct BTC-price transmission, because the ETF complex's $108.01B in net assets runs through custody arrangements concentrated at Coinbase [S5], and because qualified-custodian breadth determines how easily RIAs and advisory platforms — the documented marginal buyer cohort after the hedge-fund exodus — can scale Bitcoin allocations. A final rule that widens the custodian set and clarifies adviser obligations lowers the friction on exactly the flow channel that separates range-bound digestion from institutional reflation.

The Commission has demonstrated intent (two proposals issued in one year, an explicit substituting-rulemaking strategy ), but agency timelines slip, comment volume on a novel regime is high, and litigation risk over aggressive assertions of Bitcoin oversight could slow adoption. The observable triggers are SEC open-meeting votes and final rule releases in the Federal Register;

One caution belongs here: SEC assertion of oversight over Bitcoin itself is not unambiguously bullish. A regime that treats spot BTC as an SEC-regulated asset could add compliance cost to smaller venues and trading desks. The net price effect runs through institutional access, which is positive, but the channel is slower and smaller than legislation with statutory finality.

### GENIUS and the Stablecoin Plumbing: A January 18, 2027 Deadline

Treasury is implementing it through a proposed rulemaking on which it sought public comment [S47]; licensing obligations for stablecoin issuers take effect January 18, 2027, with smaller issuers permitted to operate under state regimes, and unlicensed stablecoins barred from US persons thereafter. [S50]

A licensed, federally chartered stablecoin sector is more bankable, more integrable with payments rails, and more likely to grow; an unlicensed or de-banked sector shrinks effective crypto liquidity. First, the January 18 licensing deadline creates a consolidation event: compliant issuers (Circle in particular) gain share while marginal issuers exit, with a short-term float contraction possible before resumed growth. [S50] Second, the New York Fed's July 2026 Liberty Street update, using the 2023 SVB–USDC depeg as its case study, flags stablecoins' vulnerability to banking stress — meaning the stablecoin channel can also propagate a liquidity shock into BTC, not just liquidity growth [S49].

This dual-edge character is why GENIUS in this report supports the range-bound base case rather than reflation: it professionalizes the plumbing without adding a marginal BTC buyer.

## 9. Scenario II — Post-Cycle Bear ($35k–$60k): The 27% Tail

First, the drawdown has already substantially happened. For the bear scenario to resolve, price must now fall a further leg from a base that has already absorbed the bulk of the historical amplitude.

The structural-demand thesis has never been tested at a cycle terminal low.
