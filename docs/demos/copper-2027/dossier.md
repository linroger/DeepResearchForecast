# Forecast the price of copper (USD per tonne, LME cash settlement) as of December 31, 2027. Establish the baseline: current LME price as of late October 2026…

## Executive Summary

The forecast statistic is the LME copper Official Settlement Price (cash), in US dollars per tonne, on 31 December 2027 — the last trading day's official close [S1][S2]. Copper enters the horizon in record territory: LME cash settled at 14,373.00 USD/t on 22 October 2026 [S1], and the IMF global price averaged 14473.0559 U.S. Dollars per Metric Ton in September 2026, up 44.806% year on year and a series record [S3], after an annual average of 9946.8819 U.S. Dollars per Metric Ton in 2025 [S4]. The central thesis is that the 2026 spike reflects a genuine concentrate bottleneck — Reuters reports Codelco's H1 2026 output fell 11% year on year to 564,000 t [S5] — amplified by collapsed treatment charges, tariff-distorted trade flows and record electrification demand, but that the marginal supply response now points to an elevated plateau rather than either a sustained further leg higher or a collapse. ICSG's 14 October 2026 projection puts the refined market in a 104,000 t surplus in 2026 widening to 368,000 t in 2027, as refined production growth accelerates to 3.4% against usage growth of 2.5% [S6] — the publisher's outlook to set the forecast against, never an observation. At roughly 24% annualized volatility [S3], one standard deviation spans the plateau band, which is why that band carries the most mass.

The canonical frame, as ranges of the settlement statistic:

- Sustained supercycle — 30% — concentrate shortage, no Cobre Panama restart and record electrification demand hold LME cash above $14,000/t at end-2027; falsified by a signed restart arrangement or recovering treatment charges.
- Elevated plateau — 40% — new supply ramps and demand growth moderates, leaving the settlement in a $10,500–$14,000/t band; falsified by a decisive, sustained break to either side.
- Mean reversion — 25% — smelter cutbacks free concentrate, Cobre Panama partially restarts and high prices curb demand, returning prices to $7,500–$10,500/t; falsified if concentrate stays scarce through 2027.
- Hard landing collapse — 5% — a recession or tariff-war demand shock alongside supply recovery drives prices below $7,500/t; requires a named macro catalyst, absent which it stays a tail.

Market-implied pricing tilts richer than this distribution: COMEX on 22 October 2026 quoted December 2026 at 6.4900 USD/lb and March 2027 at 6.5010 USD/lb [S7] — a near-flat curve holding the supercycle threshold into 2027 — while the December 2027 contract printed no trade, so no instrument directly prices the horizon [S7]. The most-likely trajectory under the modal scenario drifts from current records toward the middle of the plateau band through 2027 as Indonesian, Indian and Congolese supply ramps, without a disorderly break.

Leading indicators to watch: Panama–First Quantum restart negotiations (the largest discrete supply swing); the Antofagasta–CSPT 2027 TC/RC benchmark and CSPT cutback discipline; Cochilco's monthly Codelco and national output prints against its projected 2027 rebound; successive ICSG balance vintages; LME warehouse stocks and the cash–3M spread; the Section 232 calendar — the 25% domestic-sales requirement from 2027 and rolling Commerce/USTR additions [S8] — plus Court of International Trade litigation behind the COMEX–LME spread; and demand monitors: China State Grid investment and NDRC property policy, EV sales, datacenter construction, U.S. retail electricity sales at 412568.3744 Million kilowatt-hours in August 2026 [S9], U.S. capacity utilization at 76.85% in September 2026 [S10], and scrap supply.

## Baseline: price, inventories and market structure

### Settlement instrument and late-October level

The forecast statistic is the LME copper Official Settlement Price for cash, quoted in US dollars per tonne, contract code CA, physical settlement, lot size 25 tonnes [S2]; it is fixed in the second Ring session and published between 12:20 and 13:25 London time [S1]. LME copper trades out to 10 years, averaged over 170,000 lots daily in 2025, and delivers across 400 warehouses in 32 locations [S2]. On 22 October 2026 (data valid 22 October, published for 23 October), the LME Official Prices page shows a cash settlement of 14,373.00 USD/t with bid/offer at 14,495.00/14,496.00 USD/t [S1]. Westmetall's mirror prints for the same session (cash 14,215.00, 3M 14,096.00 USD/t; stock 272,950 t) are unconfirmed against the LME's own table [S11]. The monthly anchor is the IMF global copper price: 14,473.0559 U.S. Dollars per Metric Ton in September 2026 — a monthly mean, not a single-day close [S3].

### 2024–2026 trajectory

IMF monthly averages show 2024 opening at 8,351.3396 USD/t in January, peaking at 10,117.1635 in May and ending at 8,909.9077 in December [S3]. 2025 ran from 8,976.6804 (January) through 9,994.7732 (September) to 11,790.9641 (December), an accelerating Q4 rally [S3]. 2026 climbed to 13,552.0409 in June and 14,473.0559 in September — the highest observation in the stored 2006-10 to 2026-09 window and +44.806% year-on-year [S3].

### Inventories and the cash–3M spread

Monthly-average LME stocks moved 153,964 t (January) → 394,892 t (May peak) → 294,114 t (July) → 244,965 t (September) → 238,617 t (October 2026) [S12]. The late-October spread is small and sign-flipping: Westmetall's unconfirmed 22 October prints imply cash $119/t over 3M [S11], while combining the LME cash settlement with the 3M closing range of 14,407.72–14,510.13 USD/t implies three months roughly $35–137/t over cash [S1][S17].

### Market structure

### Volatility

Annualized volatility computed from the 239 log monthly changes of the IMF monthly copper series (window 2006-10 to 2026-09) is approximately 24% [S3]. Secondary estimates bracket it at roughly 20–30% [S22], with 28% recorded for the LME market in 1996 on a 20-day basis — the only 1990s observation available [S23]. Monthly averaging smooths daily moves, so volatility and 14-month quantiles measured on LME daily closes will be somewhat wider than these monthly-average figures.

### Large spikes and mean reversion

Rises of ≥40% over 14 months occurred three times in roughly 20 years — 2009–10 (+47%; 4,591.4605 in May 2009 to 6,750.5682 in July 2010), 2020–21 (+90%), and 2025–26 (+48%; 9,770.5804 in July 2025 to 14,473.0559 in September 2026, still open) — each preceded by or coinciding with a demand-stimulus or supply-shock regime [S3]. Both completed episodes retraced materially: the February 2011 peak of 9,880.9375 fell to 4,471.7875 by January 2016 (−55% over about five years), and the March 2022 peak of 10,230.8939 fell to 7,544.8095 by July 2022 (−26% in four months); the May 2024 local peak of 10,117.1635 fell 11% to 8,981.1177 by August 2024 [S3].

### Annual-series check

On the IMF annual series, annual-average increases of ≥+40% occurred in 2004 (+61%; 1,779.3616 → 2,863.4688), 2006 (+83%; 3,676.4947 → 6,731.3519), 2010 (+46%; 5,164.1588 → 7,538.3621) and 2021 (+51%; 6,174.5579 → 9,317.4092) [S4]; three of the four were followed by a lower annual average within 2–3 years, while 2004's rise extended through 2006 — spike years usually mean-revert, but supercycle runs can persist around three years [S4]. The stored annual observations frame the recent plateau: 6,174.5579 (2020), 9,317.4092 (2021), 8,828.9168 (2022), 8,490.7617 (2023), 9,142.1016 (2024), 9,946.8819 (2025, +8.803% year-on-year) [S4].

### Distance to trend and application

At 14,473.0559 USD/t (September 2026) [S3], the monthly price sits more than 40% above even the highest annual average of the 2021–2025 plateau, 9,946.8819 in 2025 [S4] — squarely in the ≥40%-above-trend class whose completed episodes retraced. The sample is small — two completed monthly episodes, four annual — so these base rates carry wide error bars. They nonetheless match the priors in the scenario frame: the concentration of mass in the ±1σ band behind "Elevated plateau — 40%", and the 2–3-year historical retracement behind "Mean reversion — 25%", with 2004–2006 as the template for a multi-year extension.

## Mine supply: disruptions, grades and restarts

### Chile: disruption and grade strain

Chilean supply strained through 2026. Reuters reports Codelco's H1 2026 output fell 11% year-on-year to 564,000 t (from 634,000 t) on El Teniente operational restrictions, lower Chuquicamata output and weaker grades at Ministro Hales, even as profit rose on the copper rally [S5]. May was the worst month: Codelco produced 106,300 t, −18.3% year-on-year, with Escondida down ~17% the same month, per Cochilco data relayed by The Oregon Group [S31]; July recovered only partially, to 112,800 t, −5% year-on-year [S32]. Cochilco's 2026-vintage outlook (reported October 2026) puts Chile at 5.27 Mt in 2026 (−2.6%) rebounding 5.2% in 2027 (≈5.55 Mt, derived), within world mine production of 23.5 Mt (+0.2%) in 2026 and 24.5 Mt (+3.8%) in 2027 [S28] — superseding the January 2025 vintage that had Chile peaking at 6.07 Mt in 2027 [S33].

### Peru: recovery now, additions later

Peru's July 2026 output was 236,515 t, +3.7% year-on-year (MINEM) [S34]. H1 rankings show Antamina back on top, +7.9% versus 208,734 t in H1 2025, while Cerro Verde fell 6.4% from 237,373 t [S35]. Antamina's 2026 uplift and its levelling-off thereafter are reported but unconfirmed in the fetched sources [S36]. Peru is removing 27 permit requirements and targets roughly 1 Mt of additional copper per year by 2031–32 — years beyond the horizon — with 2026 output held below the 2025 record by declining grades and social conflict [S15].

### Cobre Panama: talks proposed, no timeline

On 30 September 2026 a Panamanian government commission recommended opening formal negotiations with First Quantum on conditions to reopen the shuttered Cobre Panama mine, structured to end pending international arbitration and finance an orderly closure at no cost to the state; the report set out no scope, timing or legal structure, and President Mulino said he would study it [S37]. Reuters separately reported the commission would propose a state tie-up with First Quantum [S37]. No source in the evidence publishes restart odds or a timeline; with negotiations not yet opened as of the as-of date, restarted tonnes within 2027 would require an unusually fast agreement plus ramp.

### New-project tonnes in 2027

Ivanhoe's guidance (3 December 2025) is 380,000–420,000 t of copper at Kamoa-Kakula in 2026 and 500,000–540,000 t in 2027, still targeting ~550,000 t medium term [S38] — a derived ~+120 kt at midpoints (400→520 kt). The ramp rests on Kakula's eastern selective mining from Q1 2026, lifting mining rates to 450,000 t/month (~5.5 Mtpa annualized) by end-Q1, a new eastern mining area from mid-2026, and Project 95 completing in Q2 2026 to lift Phase 1/2 recoveries to ~95% [S38]. No evidence identifies a project named "El Tejon" with a 2027 startup; the name could not be matched to any documented project. The approved Anglo–Teck merger (close expected by March 2027; ~1.35 Mt combined annual output; Teck 2026 guidance 455,000–530,000 t versus 454,000 t in 2025) consolidates ownership rather than adding tonnes [S39][S40][S41].

### Balance implications

S&P Global estimates the 2026 concentrate deficit at about 530,000 t, and ICSG cut 2026 mine production growth to 1.6% (from 2.3%) with refined growth of just 0.4% [S15][S30]. Cochilco's 5.2% Chilean rebound and Kamoa's 2027 step-up supply the new-tonnage premise of Elevated plateau — 40%; an unresolved Cobre Panama atop the concentrate deficit is the physical core of Sustained supercycle — 30%.

## Smelting bottleneck and TC/RC collapse

### From a zero benchmark to deeply negative spot charges
The 2026 annual concentrate TC/RC benchmark between Antofagasta and Chinese smelters settled at $0 per tonne and 0 cents per pound — the lowest annual benchmark on record — set in January 2026 [S30]. Spot terms then went deeply negative: spot TC/RCs fell to -$126.80 per tonne by end-June 2026, per IEA data cited by Crux Investor [S30]; Mysteel's clean-concentrate spot index, CIF China, reached -$103.62/dmt in 2026, inflicting "severe losses" on Chinese smelters [S42]; and Argus assessed spot terms at minus US$226.2 per tonne on 18 September 2026 [S43]. The readings differ by index methodology and assessment date, but the direction is uniform and worsening: smelters now pay miners for the right to process concentrate [S30][S43].

### Smelter economics and cutback plans
Custom-smelter margins rest on TC/RCs plus sulfuric-acid byproduct credits, and both legs deteriorated through 2026 [S44]. On 22 October 2026, Bloomberg reported, citing unnamed sources, that Chinese smelters had started curbing output and told international concentrate suppliers of further cuts this year and into 2027, naming Tongling Nonferrous and Daye Nonferrous, with up to 30% of Tongling's primary smelting capacity affected [S44]. No aggregate China-wide 2027 cutback tonnage has been published; the plan consists of supplier communications rather than a CSPT quota [S44]. The CSPT had earlier announced plans to cut primary smelting capacity by more than 10% for 2026, though CRU judged a smaller decline in utilization the most plausible outcome [S45]. For broad US industrial context — a proxy, not a copper-smelter measure — total capacity utilization was 76.85% in September 2026, versus 76.046% a year earlier [S10].

### Concentrate-to-refined transmission
S&P Global estimates the 2026 concentrate deficit at about 530,000 tonnes [S15]. ICSG's October 2026 forecast accordingly holds world refined production growth to 0.4% in 2026, but sees 3.4% in 2027 as ramp-ups in Indonesia, India and the EU, better Chilean operating rates and increased scrap-based output arrive [S6]; its April 2026 forecast expected secondary refined production to grow 5.7% in 2027 [S29]. Cochilco expects world mine production of 23.5 Mt in 2026 (+0.2%) and 24.5 Mt in 2027 (+3.8%) [S28]. The end-2027 question is whether the announced cutbacks bite harder than ICSG's rebound assumes: refined growth materially below the 3.4% path would erode the projected 368,000-tonne surplus and support Sustained supercycle — 30%; delivery of the rebound fits Elevated plateau — 40%, and with a demand shock, Mean reversion — 25%.

## Demand: grids, EVs, datacenters versus construction

### Usage growth: electrification-led, decelerating
ICSG's October 2026 forecast has world refined usage growing 1.5% in 2026 and 2.5% in 2027, with Asia the main growth source; China accounts for 59% of global refined consumption, its apparent usage growing 2.5% in 2026, while rest-of-world demand is largely unchanged [S6]. ICSG attributes the growth to "demand from electrification, artificial intelligence and data centres," while noting consumption grows more modestly than processing capacity [S6]. The IEA's long-horizon framing — copper's projected 2035 supply deficit narrowed from about 30% to about 25% of demand as new projects advance — coexists with, rather than contradicts, ICSG's near-term 2027 surplus of 368,000 tonnes [S46][S6]. Critical-minerals investment fell 9% in 2025, with copper-focused spending holding up better than battery metals [S46].

### EVs: large absolute additions, slowing growth
Global electric-car sales exceeded 20 million units in 2025, up 20% year on year for a 25% market share — a fifth consecutive year of roughly +3.5 million units [S47]. China sold more than 13 million, with an annual share of almost 55%, but growth slowed to under 20% after above-75% annual rates in 2020-24 [S47]. Europe rose 30% to more than 4 million units; the US stayed just below a 10% share, with a significant Q4 fall after tax credits ended [S47]. Momentum persists into 2026: Benchmark counts 1.85 million units in July 2026, up 9% year on year, and 1.83 million in August [S48].

### Grids and datacenters
US retail electricity sales — the official load proxy, since the series has no datacenter sector — were 412,568.3744 million kWh in August 2026, up 4.602% from 394,418.8654 million kWh in August 2025 [S9]. IEA analysis frames grid investment as the key structural copper demand lever to 2027; the dollar figures sometimes cited from it (about USD 400 billion per year, needing a rise of roughly 50% by 2030, with over 2,500 GW of projects queued) are unconfirmed [S49]. No authoritative datacenter copper-tonnage estimate exists; ICSG's qualitative attribution [S6] and the EIA load series [S9] are the observable anchors.

### Construction drag, scrap and substitution
No primary-source quantification of Chinese property-sector copper use or of China State Grid's 2026-27 investment plan was available; with China at 59% of world consumption, property remains the largest unmeasured demand drag [S6]. Scrap responds on the supply side: ICSG expects secondary refined production to grow 5.7% in 2027 [S29] and includes increased scrap-based output in its 3.4% refined-growth projection [S6]. US policy now restrains scrap trade — a 25% domestic sales requirement for copper input materials from 2027 plus export controls on high-quality copper scrap [S8]. No quantified substitution estimate exists in the evidence; at record prices substitution is a stated but unmeasured risk [S46].

## US tariffs and the COMEX-LME spread

Proclamation 10962 (July 30, 2025) imposed 50% Section 232 tariffs effective August 1, 2025 on semi-finished copper products (pipes, wires, rods, sheets, tubes) and copper-intensive derivatives, levied on copper content only, while refined inputs — ores, concentrates, mattes, cathodes, anodes — and scrap were exempt [S8]. Effective April 6, 2026, the levy moved to 50% of full value for semi-finished products and 25% of full value for derivatives (10% where ≥95% of copper/steel/aluminium content is US-sourced); the inclusions process was replaced by rolling Commerce/USTR additions, a 25% domestic-sales requirement applies to copper input materials from 2027, export controls cover high-quality scrap, and USMCA-originating goods are exempt [S8]. Some summaries of the April 2026 action report a 30% semi-finished rate [S8], so the product-level rate should be resolved against the Federal Register text.

Refined cathode remains untariffed pending a White House decision [S18]. The July 2025 proclamation required a Commerce report by June 30, 2026 [S8]; Commerce recommended a phased universal refined-copper tariff of 15% from January 1, 2027, rising to 30% from January 1, 2028 [S18][S8]. No decision had been published as of late October 2026 [S19][S50]. StoneX strategist Natalie Scott-Gray calls this decision "the single biggest catalyst" facing the copper market [S18]. No copper-specific Court of International Trade ruling or calendar appears in the evidence, though the April 2026 proclamation is under general challenge.

The COMEX-LME spread narrowed from over $3,000/t in July 2025 to about $310/t in autumn 2026 [S19]; ING separately reported around $400/t [S20], so the exact late-October level is unresolved across dates and contract legs. US imports exceeded 200,000 t in July 2026, the highest monthly level in 12 years [S18], building a US stockpile reported at roughly 1.6 Mt [S19]; the tariff delay is reported to have left an ex-US deficit of about 640,000 t [S19][S51]. Against an IMF global price of 14473.0559 U.S. Dollars per Metric Ton in September 2026 (+44.806% year-on-year) [S3], the COMEX curve is near-flat: HGZ6 last traded 6.4900 USD/lb on 23 October 2026 [S24], with March 2027 at 6.5010 and May 2027 at 6.5650 USD/lb [S7] (≈$14,330/t and $14,472/t at 2,204.62 lb/t, derived).

Societe Generale's modelling of the COMEX premium over fully delivered LME metal as of mid-August 2026 implied a 14.6% probability of the 15% refined-copper tariff taking effect by January 2027 [S18]. The same modelling put the probability of a 30% duty by January 2028 at 37% [S18]. These are risk-neutral estimates, and no published probability exists for the White House decision itself.

## Key actors and incentives

With the IMF global copper price at 14,473.0559 U.S. Dollars per Metric Ton in September 2026, up 44.806% from 9,994.7732 a year earlier [S3], the actors below are managing record prices, and their decision rights — not their statements — set the 2027 path.

### Codelco and the Chilean state

Codelco answers to its state shareholder, so volume shortfalls carry fiscal cost even as the rally lifts profit. Reuters reports H1 2026 production fell 11% year on year to 564,000 tonnes (from 634,000 t) on El Teniente restrictions, lower Chuquicamata output and weaker Ministro Hales grades, even as profit rose [S5]; Cochilco data show July output of 112,800 t, down 5% [S32], after a May print of 106,300 t, down 18.3%, with Escondida down about 17% [S31]. Cochilco's 2026-vintage outlook expects Chile at 5.27 million tonnes in 2026 (−2.6%), rising 5.2% in 2027 [S28] — a recovery it must deliver after its January 2025 projection of a 6.07-million-tonne 2027 peak proved optimistic [S33]. Its incentive is to ramp output into prices it raised to US$5.95/lb for 2026 [S28].

### First Quantum versus Panama's veto

Restart rights sit with Panama. On 30 September 2026 a government commission recommended opening formal negotiations with First Quantum on conditions to reopen Cobre Panama, structured to end arbitration claims and finance an orderly closure at no cost to the state, with no scope, timing or legal structure; President Mulino said he would study the report [S37]. Reuters reported the proposed vehicle is a state tie-up with First Quantum [S37]. With no published timeline, 2027 output looks improbable, and any deal is framed around financing closure — the restart is Mulino's option, not First Quantum's.

### Chinese smelters and the CSPT

Rights are split between individual firms and CSPT coordination. The 2026 Antofagasta benchmark settled at $0 per tonne and 0 cents per pound, a record low [S30]; spot terms are deeply negative, with Argus at −$226.2/t on 18 September 2026 [S43]. Bloomberg reports Tongling and Daye processing less concentrate, up to 30% of Tongling's primary capacity affected, and cuts planned into 2027 [S44]. The announced-cut track record counsels discounting: CSPT flagged a >10% primary-capacity cut for 2026, but CRU judged the most plausible outcome a smaller decline in utilisation [S45]. Antofagasta's shift to spot/index-linked settlements [S55] moves pricing power toward miners.

### US trade authorities

The President holds Section 232 decision rights with a record of speed: 144 days from the 10 March 2025 investigation to 50% tariffs on semi-finished copper content effective 1 August 2025, refined cathode exempt [S8]; from 6 April 2026, 50% of full value on semi-finished products and 25% on derivatives, plus a 25% domestic-sales requirement from 2027 and scrap export controls [S8][S52]. Commerce recommended a 15% refined tariff from 1 January 2027 and 30% from 1 January 2028 [S8][S18]; no decision is published as of late 2026 [S19][S50]. Effects are visible: July 2026 imports above 200,000 tonnes, a 12-year high [S18][S19]; a US stockpile reported near 1.6 million tonnes [S19]; and a COMEX–LME spread narrowed from over $3,000/t in July 2025 to about $310/t [S19], against ING's roughly $400/t [S20] — the exact late-October level is unconfirmed. StoneX's Natalie Scott-Gray calls the refined-copper decision the single biggest catalyst facing the market [S18]. Societe Generale's risk-neutral modelling of the COMEX premium over fully delivered LME metal as of mid-August 2026 implied a 14.6% probability of the 15% refined tariff by January 2027 and a 37% probability of the 30% duty by January 2028 [S18].

## Scenarios and probabilities

### Scenario Probability Distribution
- **Sustained supercycle (30% probability):** Concentrate shortage, no Cobre Panama restart and record electrification demand keep the market in deficit, holding LME cash above $14,000/t at end-2027; prior: copper 20-25% annual vol makes sustained highs possible after a supply-driven spike.
- **Elevated plateau (40% probability):** New supply ramps and demand growth moderates, leaving prices in a $10,500-$14,000/t band at end-2027 with elevated but not extreme levels; prior: ±1 sigma bin around spot under 20-25% vol carries the most mass.
- **Mean reversion (25% probability):** Smelter cutbacks free concentrate, Cobre Panama partially restarts and high prices crush demand, returning prices to $7,500-$10,500/t; prior: copper spikes above trend have historically retraced within 2-3 years.
- **Hard landing collapse (5% probability):** A global recession or a tariff-war demand shock hits alongside supply recovery, driving prices below $7,500/t; prior: tail bin under copper volatility, requiring a named macro catalyst.

The forecast statistic is the LME Official Settlement price for cash copper, in US$ per tonne, on 31 December 2027, published by the LME at the mid-day fixing [S1][S2]. The verifiable monthly anchor is the IMF global price: 14473.0559 U.S. Dollars per Metric Ton (2026-09), +44.806% year on year and a series record [S3]; the LME price page quotes 22 October 2026 cash settlement at 14,373.00 USD/t [S1]. Calculated 14-month volatility on the monthly series is ≈24%, so ±1 sigma ≈ ±26% — roughly $10,600–18,100/t around that quote [S3].

### Sustained supercycle — 30%
Causal path: a 2026 concentrate deficit of about 530,000 t (S&P Global estimate) [S15] holds spot treatment charges deeply negative (Argus: −US$226.2/t on 18 September 2026) [S43] and forces Chinese smelter cuts into 2027 [S44], while electrification and datacentre demand grows [S6] and no Cobre Panama restart occurs — only negotiations were recommended, on 30 September 2026 [S37]; the IEA still projects a 2035 supply gap [S46]. Falsifier: ICSG's refined output +3.4% and 368,000 t surplus in 2027 materializing with TCs recovering [S6].

### Elevated plateau — 40%
Kamoa-Kakula guides 500,000–540,000 t for 2027 [S38], Cochilco sees Chilean output +5.2% [S28] and ICSG a widening surplus [S6], capping prices while structural demand floors them. The near-flat COMEX curve to H1-2027 (≈$14,330–14,470/t derived from quoted legs) [S7] and the ±1-sigma base rate [S3] place most mass here. Falsifier: backwardation above $14,000/t persisting through H2 2027 on draining stocks.

### Mean reversion — 25%
Smelter cutbacks free concentrate, a partial Cobre restart [S37] and high-price substitution return the market to surplus [S6]; the IMF annual series averaged 8490.7617 (2023), 9142.1016 (2024) and 9946.8819 (2025), so spot sits well above the recent plateau [S4]. Both completed ≥40%/14-month spikes retraced materially [S3] and 3 of 4 annual ≥+40% years were followed by lower averages within 2–3 years [S4]; tariff rejection would unwind the COMEX premium [S19].

### Hard landing collapse — 5%
Requires a named macro catalyst atop supply recovery; the only class analogue is the ≈−42% GFC move [S3]. US capacity utilization of 76.85% (2026-09) shows no recession signal [S10], though US EV sales fell in Q4 2025 after tax credits ended [S47].
