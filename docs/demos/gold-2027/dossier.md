## Executive Summary

The forecast resolves on the LBMA Gold Price auction in US dollars per troy ounce, at the last trading day's official close of 2027. The baseline is a market in correction after a historic run: spot was quoted at $4,099.20 on 21 October 2026 [S1] (Macrotrends' daily composite printed $4,132 the same day [S2]), down 5.2% year on year [S2] and well below the LBMA a.m. auction record of $5,501.70 set on 29 January 2026 [S3]. The central thesis is that this correction reflects a real-yield shock, not a breakdown in gold's demand base — and that the two forces will keep contesting the price through 2027.

On the bearish side, the Fed has stopped easing: the US policy rate stood at 3.875% per year at end-September 2026 [S4], and the 10-year TIPS yield averaged 2.638% in September 2026, the top of its five-year range [S5]. Realised inflation compounds the squeeze risk: CPI was 3.717% year over year in September 2026 [S6], far above the 10-year breakeven of 2.351% for the same month [S7] — a wedge that either feeds the hedge bid or, if it closes through disinflation and higher real rates, removes it. On the supportive side, the floor is unusually firm: global gold ETF holdings reached a record 4,256 tonnes in September 2026, absorbing roughly US$10bn of inflows into a falling price [S8], central banks bought a net 289 tonnes in Q2 2026 — a record for a second quarter [S9] — and record H1 2026 mine output of 1,867 tonnes has not capped the market [S10].

The scenario frame assigns: Base: grind higher — 45% — gradual Fed cuts, drifting real yields and 500–800t/year of official buying put end-2027 between $4,300 and $5,200; Bull: policy and crisis premium — 25% — a recession, geopolitical or debt-crisis shock drives aggressive easing and gold above $5,500; Bear: real-yield squeeze — 20% — sticky inflation forces holds or hikes, lifting real yields and the dollar, leaving gold near or below $3,800; Range-bound consolidation — 10% — positioning exhaustion caps gold between $3,800 and $4,500.

The resulting distribution: P10 lies inside the bear mass at or below $3,800; cumulative weight passes roughly 30% near $4,500, placing P50 in the lower half of the base band; and P90 must clear Bull's $5,500 floor, consistent with the January record [S3] and the $6,000 end-2026 targets J.P. Morgan and Deutsche Bank carried as publisher forecasts [S11]. Institutional 2027 year-end targets clustering at $5,000–$5,600 [S12] are publisher outlooks to position the forecast against, and sit near this frame's P75.

The indicators that matter most: the 28 October 2026 FOMC, with market-implied odds of 83.9% on a hold [S13] and 73.3% on ending December at 4.00–4.25% [S14]; the gap between the September 2026 SEP median, an FOMC projection holding 4.25% through 2028 [S15], and the roughly 77bp of cumulative tightening markets price through October 2027 [S16]; monthly World Gold Council central-bank and ETF tonnage [S9][S8][S17]; and the 10-year TIPS yield itself [S5] as the cleanest discriminator between Base and Bear.

## Historical base rates for gold price moves

### Basis

Base rates here are calendar-year changes computed on year-end closes of the LBMA Gold Price, the administered benchmark for this forecast; the LBMA's Q2 2026 report is the source of auction prints such as the $5,501.70 a.m. price of 29 January 2026 [S3]. Macrotrends figures use its own daily intraday composite, which is why its 21 October 2026 level of $4,132/oz sits slightly above the World Gold Council's $4,099.20 spot quote for the same day [S2][S1].

### Calendar-year return distribution

Across 2000–2025 the average calendar-year return was approximately 10.9%, a cumulative gain of roughly +1,075%, per a Visual Capitalist chart summary relayed via a secondary aggregator [S34]. Negative calendar years occur in roughly 7 of the 30 years 1996–2025 — commonly cited instances are 1997, 2000, 2014, 2015, 2018, 2021 and 2022 — an indicative count from general market history rather than a confirmed year-by-year table [S2]. The recent cycle:

|---|---|---|
| 2024 | over +25%, closing at $2,610.85/oz, strongest annual performance in 14 years | LBMA Q4 2024 report [S20] |
| 2025 | roughly +65%, derived from $2,610.85 [S20] to an implied ~$4,320 end-2025 close (Macrotrends' $4,132 on 21 Oct 2026 and −4.4% YTD) [S2] | derived |
| 2026 (to 21 Oct) | −4.4% YTD, and 5.2% below the $4,359 print a year earlier | Macrotrends [S2] |

Two end-2025 anchors disagree: the derived ~$4,320 versus goldprice.org's $4,083.55 on 31 December 2025, likely a spot close rather than the p.m. auction print [S23]. LBMA's own Q4/FY 2025 report states gold exceeded $4,000 for the first time ever in 2025, gaining over 10% in Q4 alone [S21], and Newmont's SEC-filed annual report lists a 2025 low of $2,633/oz and high of $4,449/oz [S22], both corroborating a late-year surge. Five-year compound growth has run at 18.3% per year, per Macrotrends [S2].

### Post-surge 12-month behaviour

Years with calendar gains above 40% since 1996 form a reference class of exactly one — 2025 — with no fully resolved 12-month follow-through; a Laplace-adjusted (k+1)/(n+2) rate is directionally uninformative at n=1 [S2]. The partial record shows severe mean reversion rather than continuation: from the 29 January 2026 peak, gold fell nearly 27% in five months to close June 2026 at $4,026.05 (p.m.), touching $3,994.50 (a.m.) on 25 June 2026 — below $4,000 for the first time since early November 2025 [S3] — and on 21 October 2026 stood 23.2% below the $5,379 record close [S2]. A sub-$4,000 print within months of an all-time high shows that the "Bear: real-yield squeeze" outcome (20%, gold ending 2027 near or below $3,800) and the "Range-bound consolidation" outcome (10%, $3,800–$4,500) already sit within one year's realized range. For calibration, base rates favour a 2027 central tendency of low-double-digit gains over a repeat of 2025's >40% surge, while the n=1 conditional class caps confidence in any directional post-surge rate.

## Geopolitical and policy risk premium

### The inflation configuration

US headline CPI inflation was 3.717% (year-over-year change) in September 2026, up from a 2026 low of 2.391% in January and below its 4.167% May peak [S6]. Market expectations stayed anchored: the 10-year breakeven inflation rate averaged 2.351% in September 2026 and stood at 2.37% on 21 October 2026 [S7], while University of Michigan one-year consumer expectations were 4% in August 2026, off a 4.8% May peak [S31]. The FOMC's September 2026 projection puts PCE inflation at 3.7% in 2026 falling only to 2.4% in 2027 [S15]. The wedge between realised inflation near 3.7% and priced expectations near 2.35% is the core tension: disinflation removes the hedge bid, while re-acceleration feeds both hedging demand and the hawkish Fed response that defines Bear: real-yield squeeze (20%).

## Supply side: mine output and recycling

Mine output is running at record levels. World Gold Council Gold Demand Trends data show Q2 2026 mine production up 2% y/y at 965.6t, an all-time Q2 high in WGC data back to 2000, within record H1 2026 output of 1,867t, up 3% on 1,808t in H1 2025 [S10]; the WGC supply page also reports a record Q1 2026 of 885t, up 2% y/y [S54]. Growth is concentrated in a few ramp-ups: Canada +29% y/y (Agnico Eagle's Detour Lake), Chile +24% (Gold Fields' Salares Norte, Rio2's Fenix), Burkina Faso +17% (West African Resources' Kiaka, Orezone's Bombore) and Ghana +8% (Newmont's Ahafo North) [S10]. By-product economics are supportive: the global copper price, a driver of by-product gold from copper mines, stood at 13542.8209 U.S. Dollars per Metric Ton in July 2026, near the top of its five-year stored range of 12528.7095 (2026-03) to 13552.0409 (2026-06) [S33].

### Recycling: the price-elastic swing source

Per the WGC supply page, recycling rose 5% y/y in Q1 2026 on higher prices [S54], then fell 6% y/y to 326.1t in Q2 [S10] as prices weakened — gold traded below $4,000 on 25 June 2026 ($3,994.50 a.m.) for the first time since early November 2025 [S3]. The scrap response into 2027 is not established in the evidence; the Q2 pattern suggests holders defer selling into dips, so recycling releases metal mainly into price spikes.

### Scenario signatures and signposts

With mine output at records, supply is second-order in all four canonical scenarios; prices are being set on the demand side. Under Bull: policy and crisis premium — 25% — a spike toward the January 2026 high would likely trigger a recycling surge that partially self-corrects the move; under Bear: real-yield squeeze — 20% — and Range-bound consolidation — 10% — weak prices keep scrap subdued, marginally tightening the balance; under Base: grind higher — 45% — steady mine growth near the 2-3% pace with price-responsive scrap implies broadly flat total supply. The key monitorable is the WGC quarterly supply table (next: Q3 2026 data, due end-October 2026): renewed y/y recycling growth alongside record mine output would signal a loosening balance, while continued scrap contraction despite spot above $4,000 [S1][S2] would signal exhaustion of near-market scrap, tightening the balance.
