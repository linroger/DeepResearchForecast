# Forecast the price of copper (USD per tonne, LME cash settlement) as of December 31, 2027, with a probability-weighted central forecast (P10/P50/P90 levels) -…

## Executive Summary

This report forecasts the LME Grade A copper cash settlement, in USD per tonne, on 31 December 2027. The central thesis: a concentrate-led squeeze — Chinese smelting overcapacity pressing against scarce mine supply, not demand strength — is setting the price level, and it is more likely to persist into late 2027 than to dissipate. The probability-weighted centre therefore sits in the mid-$14,000s, close to the market's own horizon anchor, the LME December 2027 prompt at $14,650/t on 14 October 2026 against cash of $14,751/t [S1]. The frame places the median inside the $13,000–16,000 band (the market-implied mid sits there too), the P10 just below $11,000 (the 15% Demand recession mass) and the P90 approaching $18,000 (the 15% breakout mass), with fatter downside tails than a lognormal curve implies.

The baseline is a record regime, not a normal one. The Global price of Copper averaged 14473.0559 U.S. Dollars per Metric Ton in 2026-09, up 44.806% year on year [S2]; Goldman dates the record at $13,387/t on 6 January 2026 [S3], though the exact record print is unconfirmed. Stocks are extreme but polarised: COMEX warehouses held more than 714,000 t on 14 October 2026 — roughly 70% of visible global stocks — against LME stocks of 229,650 t [S4] (Westmetall prints 273,750 t on 23 October [S5]), leaving combined exchange cover near 0.4 months of ICSG's expected 2026 world refined consumption of 28.59 Mt [S6] (derived). The squeeze gauge is unambiguous: SMM's imported concentrate index stood at −$231.68/dmt on 30 September 2026 [S7], smelters effectively paying for feed.

The mechanism to 2027: ICSG cut 2026 mine-production growth to 1.3% and projects 3.1% for 2027 [S6] — a projection resting on a Cobre Panama restart plus the Kamoa-Kakula, QB2 and other ramps — while Chinese refined output, up 4% y/y in January–August 2026 [S8], still runs against November 2025's CSPT pledge to cut primary output by more than 10% [S9]; smelter discipline is the binding uncertainty on refined supply.

Four mutually exclusive end-state regimes frame the distribution: Squeeze persists — 40% — the concentrate shortage keeps ex-US refined tight and copper holds $13,000–16,000; Supply catches up — 30% — restarts, ramps and capacity discipline flip the balance to surplus and ease prices toward $11,000–13,000; Supercycle breakout — 15% — tariff escalation, SRB buying or a supply shock drives copper above $18,000; Demand recession — 15% — a Chinese or global downturn pulls it below $11,000.

Leading indicators to watch: the 2027 Antofagasta–Jiangxi benchmark TC/RC settlement, whose window opens weeks after the as-of date; SMM weekly spot TCs, where persistence below zero versus recovery toward zero separates Squeeze persists from Supply catches up; ICSG's spring 2027 revision of its 3.1% mine-growth projection [S6]; the Cobre Panama and Section 232 timelines, with the US tariff sunset falling exactly on the forecast date; release of the COMEX overhang [S4] versus LME draws and the cash-3M backwardation ($545/t on 17 August 2026, easing to $176/t [S10]); and Q4 2026 Chinese smelter maintenance against the scrap-supply crackdown [S8].

## Smelting capacity and concentrate shortage

### The concentrate signal
The 2026 annual concentrate benchmark between Antofagasta and major Chinese smelters settled in January 2026 at $0/t TC and 0.0 ¢/lb RC, the lowest ever recorded [S7][S21]. Spot terms have since spiralled deeper negative — smelters effectively pay miners for feed [S9] — and the trajectory through October 2026 is unambiguous:

CRU notes the spot-minus-contract gap averaged $12 over 2000–2023 versus roughly $90 in 2024–25 [S7].

### Why curtailments lagged the signal
Chinese refined output still grew 4% year on year in January–August 2026 [S8], and despite the CSPT's November 2025 pledge to cut 2026 output by 10% [S9], reported output was up 7.4% year on year through April 2026 [S37]. Byproduct economics explain the defiance: sulphuric acid (the SMM China smelting acid index hit 1,665 yuan/mt, up 83.7% from the start of 2026) and gold/silver credits offset negative TCs [S20][S9]. Those cushions are eroding — acid prices fell 11% in September 2026 (Oilchem), China suspended acid exports for eight months from May 2026, and a tax crackdown is shrinking scrap feed, tightening both feed streams at once [S8][S20].

### Whether curtailments bind in 2027
Wood Mackenzie and Zijin Tianfeng Futures expect full-year 2026 Chinese refined growth of only 3–3.4%, the lowest since at least 2000 versus 10.4% in 2025, with a sharp Q4 fall [S8]; seven smelters plan 30–60 days of maintenance in October–November 2026, removing roughly 80,000 t (Zhuochuang) [S8]. September accidents at Escondida, Las Bambas and El Teniente deepened feed losses [S8]. ICSG's October 2026 forecast, as reported second-hand, has world refined production up just 0.4% in 2026 — primary electrolytic output declining on scarce concentrate — then +3.4% in 2027, driven by smelter ramp-ups in Indonesia, India and the EU [S6][S14]; its April release projected secondary refined output growing 5.7% in 2027 versus 2.3% for primary, making scrap the marginal feed [S38]. China's September 2026 concentrate imports of 2.48 Mt, down 4.1% year on year, corroborate the shortage [S39]; nameplate capacity additions and blister/anode constraints remain unquantified in the available evidence. The binding constraint on 2027 refined supply is therefore concentrate and smelter economics, not demand: under "Squeeze persists — 40%" curtailments cap refined output despite demand growth, while under "Supply catches up — 30%" capacity discipline plus ICSG's 3.1% 2027 mine-growth forecast lets TCs rebuild toward zero [S14]. The nearest test is the 2027 benchmark negotiation at Asia Copper Week, 17–19 November 2026, framed by Novaex as a referendum on the benchmark system itself after Antofagasta's provisional mid-year shift toward index-linked pricing [S7][S40].

ICSG cut 2026 usage for Middle East conflict uncertainty and trade-flow disruption, attributing its flip to surplus to lower usage and higher secondary refined production [S38]; January–August 2026 already shows a +218 kt refined surplus (+181 kt bonded-adjusted) [S17].

### Price-elastic offsets
The bridge operates in a record price regime: the official series shows Global price of Copper at 14473.0559 U.S. Dollars per Metric Ton (2026-09) versus 9994.7732 U.S. Dollars per Metric Ton (2025-09), a change of +4478.2827 (+44.806%) [S2]. At these levels scrap responds fastest — it uses ~65% less energy in secondary refining — and the Q4 2026 tax crackdown tightens exactly this stream [S41][S8]. Bank of America expects demand up 10% to 30.32 Mt by 2030 with a 1.84 Mt deficit that year [S41], a long-run view in tension with ICSG's near-term surpluses of 104 kt (2026) and 368 kt (2027) [S6].

### Net read
Demand growth is positive but decelerating, concentrated in electrification builds: the confirmed datacentre increment (~65–80 kt/yr) is small against ~700 kt/yr gross usage growth, with grid and EV projections, if they hold, covering most of the remainder. China's slowdown from 4–5% toward 2% is the largest single swing within the total [S44]. "Squeeze persists — 40%" requires this demand to hold while refined supply stays capped; "Demand recession — 15%" turns on a Chinese-led slump. The dated falsifier is ICSG's April 2027 forecast round: a 2027 usage-growth revision below ~1.5% would mark the downside path.

## Stocks, SRB and US tariff policy

### Inventory cover: stranded West, tight East

As of October 14, 2026, COMEX-approved US warehouses held roughly 70% of visible global copper stocks, after inventories rose nearly 750% since February 2025 to more than 714,000 tonnes [S4]. LME stocks stood at 229,650 tonnes in mid-October 2026 [S4]; Westmetall's table prints 273,750 tonnes for October 23, 2026 [S5] — a date and definition gap between secondary citations of LME data, not a dispute over the direction. Against ICSG's expected 2026 world refined consumption of 28.59 Mt (per The Oregon Group's reading of ICSG tables) [S6], combined exchange stocks give roughly 0.4 months of cover, the LME leg under 0.1 month (derived: 229,650 t plus more than 714,000 t over 28.59 Mt/yr) [S4][S6]. The paradox: ICSG's October 14, 2026 forecasts of a 104,000 t surplus in 2026 and 368,000 t in 2027 [S4], with a +218 kt January–August 2026 surplus already recorded [S17], coexist with ex-US tightness because tariff-dislocated metal is stranded in US warehouses [S4].

### Section 232 and the COMEX–LME arbitrage

Proclamation 10962 imposed 50% Section 232 copper tariffs effective July 30, 2025 (Federal Register August 5, 2025) [S45]. Effective April 6, 2026, rates became 50% on semi-finished copper products and 25% on copper-intensive derivatives (10% if at least 95% of content is US-sourced), with rolling additions authorized — a standing lever with no fixed deadline [S45]. A temporary floating-tariff framework (Annex III) runs through December 31, 2027, an expiry landing exactly on the forecast horizon [S46]. The arbitrage has largely closed: the COMEX–LME spread narrowed from over $3,000/t in July 2025 to about $310/t by mid-October 2026, with roughly 100,000 t still due to arrive in the US through October 2026 per Wood Mackenzie's Charles Cooper [S4]. COMEX-linked US copper costs hit a record $15,057/t while LME cash averaged $14,566/t in October 2026 month-to-date, up from $13,089 in January 2026 [S4]; the official monthly series prints 14473.0559 U.S. Dollars per Metric Ton for 2026-09, +44.806% year on year [S2].

### Stockpilers

CNIA, the state-backed association, recommended in February 2026 expanding state copper reserves and concentrate buffers [S47]; the reserves administration made price inquiries for copper and other metals in March 2025, per Bloomberg via Mining.com [S48]. The Chinese stockpiler's record is countercyclical: 2021 auctions released copper in batches of 20,000–30,000 t to cool prices [S49][S50][S51]. The US "Project Vault" program (about $12 billion) has begun buying — Mercuria delivered 12,000 t of DRC copper, reported [S52] — though Wood Mackenzie estimates a 60-day copper supply alone would cost $4.9 billion [S47]. Deutsche Bank's Daniel Ghali projects the US and China could together hold 71% of global copper inventories within a year, reported [S53]. At 14473.0559 USD/t (2026-09) [S2], outright Chinese buying would run against the 2021 pattern; the marginal stockpiling bid currently sits in Washington.

## Actors and decision points

### Chinese smelters: the binding constraint

The CSPT pledged in November 2025 to cut primary smelting output by more than 10% in 2026 [S9]. Compliance has been partial: output rose 7.4% y/y through April 2026, reported [S37], and refined output grew 4% y/y in January–August 2026 [S8]. Wood Mackenzie and Zijin Tianfeng see 2026 growth of only 3–3.4%, the lowest since at least 2000, versus 10.4% in 2025 [S8]. The constraint is now feed and byproducts: seven smelters scheduled 30–60 days of maintenance for October–November 2026, cutting about 80,000 t [S8]; analysts expect a Q4 2026 drop as a tax crackdown tightens scrap supply [S8]; sulphuric-acid prices fell 11% in September 2026 [S8] and China suspended acid exports from May 2026 for eight months [S20]. Gold and silver byproducts still cushion negative TCs [S9].

### Benchmark negotiators

The 2026 Antofagasta–Chinese smelters benchmark settled at $0/t TC and 0 cents/lb RC in January 2026, the lowest ever [S7]. The SMM Imported Copper Concentrate Index read −$231.68/dmt on September 30, 2026 [S7]; Fastmarkets assessed spot at −US$272.20/t on October 9, 2026, reported [S6]. The 2027 benchmark is decided around Asia Copper Week, November 17–19, 2026 [S7], with CRU reporting a drift to index-linked terms after provisional mid-year settlements [S40] — the market's nearest dated decision.

### Miners and governments

## Scenario frame and probabilities

### Scenario Probability Distribution
- **Squeeze persists (40% probability):** Concentrate shortage and smelter curtailments keep the refined market in deficit, stocks stay tight and copper holds or extends above the $13,000-16,000 regime by end-2027; prior: copper 20-25% annual volatility around an elevated spot.
- **Supply catches up (30% probability):** Cobre Panama restart plus project ramp-ups and Chinese capacity discipline flip the balance to surplus per ICSG 2027, easing prices back toward $11,000-13,000; prior: deficits historically resolve within 18-24 months of high prices.
- **Supercycle breakout (15% probability):** Tariff escalation, SRB buying or a major supply shock plus demand acceleration drives copper above $18,000; prior: ~1-sigma tail under 20-25% volatility from a $14,400 base.
- **Demand recession (15% probability):** A global or Chinese downturn plus high-price demand destruction pulls copper below $11,000; prior: ~1-sigma downside tail.

The four scenarios are mutually exclusive end-state regimes — settlement levels on 31 December 2027, not touch points — separated by one question: does the concentrate shortage keep transmitting into ex-US refined tightness, or does it dissipate first. The anchor: the IMF Global price of Copper averaged 14473.0559 U.S. Dollars per Metric Ton in 2026-09, up 44.806% year on year [S2], and the LME December 2027 prompt closed at $14,650/t on 14 October 2026 per The Industry Spread quoting LME prices [S1] — a market mid inside the Squeeze persists band and above Supply catches up's $11,000-13,000, so market-implied central odds align with the heavier regime, though the print is ten days stale [S1]. Base rates first: in the stored monthly series, 12-month moves of ±40% or more occurred in at least four episodes across 20 years; a 579 kt deficit did not stop the fall to the 2022-07 low of 7544.8095 USD/t [S2][S17]; the 2008 demand shock cut about 64% peak-to-trough in eight months [S2].

Spot TCs at −$272.20/t on 2026-10-09 (Fastmarkets) [S6] and an SMM weekly index of −$231.68/dmt on 2026-09-30 (Novaex) [S7] cap Chinese refined growth at 3-3.4% in 2026, the lowest since at least 2000 [S8], with seven smelters cutting ~80,000 t of output in Oct-Nov maintenance [S8]; COMEX warehouses hold ~70% of visible stocks, LME 229,650 t [S4]. ### Supply catches up (30%)
