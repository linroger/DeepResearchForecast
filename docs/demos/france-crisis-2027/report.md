## Part 2 — Framework & Synthesis

### Framework

Four questions organise this forecast, and they resolve in a fixed causal order. Second, does the Lecornu government survive the censure motion that follows any use of Article 49.3? [S28] Fourth, given the answers, does the presidential election proceed on schedule on 18 April 2027, and in what political configuration? [S64] The scenario probabilities in Part 1 are the joint probabilities of these four chains, not independent point forecasts stitched together.

Confidence is medium, not high. The framework's weakness is that its three named scenarios are correlated through a single variable — the conservative right's willingness to keep the government alive — and shocks to that variable move two scenarios at once.

### Causal Logic of the Modal Path

**Budget procedure.** Every budget since the 2024 snap dissolution has been enacted only through Article 49.3. [S28]

**Censure arithmetic.** The 1958–present base rate is decisive: only one censure motion has been adopted since the founding of the Fifth Republic. [S39] Abstention is the rational strategy for deputies who want to criticise the budget without triggering its collapse.

The observed general-government net borrowing for France was -5.758% of GDP in 2024 [S8], and the publisher projections run -5.065% for 2025 (estimate), -5.352% for 2026 and -5.224% for 2027 [S8].

Within that baseline, Le Pen qualifying first reflects structural polling strength and a fragmented opposition field, not a fiscal judgement;

### Causal Logic of the Downside Scenarios

Against: French urban unrest has historically peaked and receded within weeks, and insured-loss escalation above the October 2026 episode requires a second shock of comparable magnitude — the recurrence base rate is low. [S64] For: riot risk is seasonal (spring and summer), the October grievances remain unaddressed, and a caretaker configuration is precisely the state in which preventive repression fails. The probability is deliberately below the naive recurrence rate because the scenario requires the *full* chain — riots *and* budget failure — not the first link alone.

It closes only if the Socialist Party's margin flips, and the PS has abstained-or-supported precisely to keep the NFP from owning an early-election disaster. Two things would have to change simultaneously: the PS must conclude that Lecornu's austerity is worse than a Mélenchon-led transition, and enough non-left deputies must accept that transition. Our scenario analysis judges both conditions jointly unlikely; the 1958 base rate of adopted censure motions (one in seven decades) caps this scenario well below the escalation path, even though its trigger is a single roll-call vote rather than a street-level cascade. [S39] The NFP-endorsed candidate finishing first or second follows mechanically if censure passes — it is the downstream clause, not an independent bet.

This bin is not a hedge; it is a statement that boundary cases dominate the outcome space of a six-month French political forecast.

### Decisive Evidence

Three pieces of evidence carry the most weight in defending the spine.

First, the censure base rate. we do not make that argument, because the same Assembly has already failed to censure on multiple occasions for exactly the predicted reason — opposition mutually deterrence.

Second, the deficit data's internal consistency.

Third, the macro backdrop is benign enough to keep the political economy stable. Weak-but-positive growth is the regime in which muddle-through is easiest: bad enough to keep the deficit contested, good enough to prevent the recession-plus-spread-spiral that the escalation scenario would need as fiscal reinforcement.

### Weighing the Prediction-Market Anchors

The Polymarket snapshot is thin for our purposes: the three liquid candidate-winners markets — Royal at 0%, Faure at 0%, Panot at 0% — price individual left candidates winning the presidency at effectively zero. Volume is substantial (roughly USD 6.4–7.0 million each), so these are not stale quotes.

We use them in three ways. First, as a cross-check on the Censure and Left Surge scenario: markets putting every named left-of-centre candidate at 0% to *win* is consistent with our 12% ceiling on an NFP-first-or-second path — a candidate can finish second in the first round at 0% to win overall, so there is no contradiction, but the market's conviction that no left candidate wins at all reinforces our judgment that the left surge is the least likely named path. Third, as a discipline: where our probabilities diverge from market aggregates by more than ten points on overlapping questions, the divergence must be argued. On the questions these markets actually price, we do not diverge.

The markets' limits matter more than their prices here. They price the election, not the budget, not censure votes, and not public order — the three variables our spine actually turns on.

### Defending the Spine's Numbers

The spine's internal consistency is worth stating explicitly, because the Part-1 binary probabilities were derived from these joint scenarios, not the reverse.

We hold it above a pure recurrence base rate because the scenario analysis identifies a genuine feedback channel the base rate misses: a slipped budget weakens the government's order-keeping credibility, and weakened policing raises recurrence odds. The cascade is self-amplifying only once the first link fires; hence a probability between the low recurrence base rate and the high conditional cascade rate.

We hold it down because the PS's abstention incentive survives austerity: voting censure hands the pre-election narrative to Mélenchon and forces PS deputies to defend an NFP budget record they never wrote. Incentive analysis beats headline volatility here.

No evidence encountered in drafting requires adjusting the assigned probabilities. The spine stands: muddle-through as the modal path, a public-order cascade as the principal risk, an arithmetic flip as the tail, and a wide residual honest about the boundary cases that dominate six-month political forecasting.

## Executive Forecast Overview: Four Paths from October 2026 to the Ballot Box

This executive overview states the report's core forecast, explains how the four crises interlock, and sets out the four paths — with probabilities and objective resolution criteria — that the detailed chapters which follow will expand.

### The Core Forecast: Muddle-Through Is Modal, Not Comfortable

Overall confidence is **medium**, and deliberately so: the modal path's probability embeds roughly even odds that at least one of its pillars bends before April.

The forecast rests on three structural facts that recur throughout every chapter of this report. First, since the 2024 dissolution produced a hung National Assembly, **every French budget has passed only through Article 49.3** — the constitutional device that lets a government enact law unless an absolute majority censures it. [S28] Only one censure motion has been adopted since 1958 [S39]. Whatever happens in the streets or the Assembly, France votes on 18 April 2027. [S66]

### Four Interlocking Crises, One Causal Chain

What makes this forecast unusually tractable — and unusually fragile — is that the four domains are not independent risks. They form a single causal chain, and this report treats them accordingly. 49.3 fuses the budget's fate to the government's survival, because censure following an engagement voids the bill; [S28]

The historical base rate is the reason recurrence, not recurrence's absence, deserves the analytical weight. Of four prior severe French riot episodes since 2005, **three were followed by another severe episode within five years, at a shrinking interval — roughly three years from the 2023 riots to October 2026**. [S17] Yet the base rate is not destiny, and the report steelmans the counter-case: the October de-escalation was fast and effective, with Lecornu crediting the curfews with restoring calm and holding a state of emergency in reserve "if needed" [S51], and the first post-riot poll showed presidential voting intentions largely stable [S57]. Contained shocks do not become regime shocks merely by repetition of the word "crisis."

The election still happens on time — this path predicts an election held under deteriorating conditions that structurally favour the front-runner, not a postponement. Chapter 3 prices it.

The scenario requires the PS to abandon abstention at precisely the moment when doing so strengthens LFI's presidential positioning, with Mélenchon already tracked toward a runoff against Le Pen [S54]. Chapter 4 explains why the triggers are real but the actor alignment is absent.

Partial combinations — a passed budget followed by riot recurrence, a survived censure followed by deficit slippage, a same-bloc prime-ministerial succession by resignation — are neither clean muddle-through nor clean escalation, and this report refuses to force them into named boxes.

This report prices it below 5%.

### Why the Modal Path Is Narrow — and How to Tell If It Breaks

Because the four domains share decision nodes, a small dated calendar resolves most of the uncertainty. In order of proximity: **Moody's review of Aa3/Negative on 23 October 2026** — two days after this report's as-of date; [S66] **S&P's A+/Stable review on 27 November**; [S66] the **Commission's Autumn forecast** in November; France Assureurs' **first official riot-loss estimate**; and, into the new year, curfew orders, catastrophe declarations, the Cour des comptes audit and the spread itself. the market-path conditioning is developed in Chapter 9; the riot-loss economics and emergency-powers law in Chapter 6;

One counterintuitive asymmetry frames everything that follows. The deeper the budget mess, the *more* likely enactment becomes — because the only actors who can kill the bill via censure are the same actors who have declined to do so five times running. And the worse the street violence, the *more* likely the election is held exactly on schedule — because in the French constitutional order, the ballot box, not the emergency decree, is the only exit from a scenario like this one. France between October 2026 and April 2027 is therefore most likely to experience not resolution but repetition: a government that governs without a majority, a budget that passes without a vote, riots that recur or do not, and an election that happens on time in a country on edge. That is the modal forecast. The chapters that follow test it, price its alternatives, and specify exactly what would prove it wrong.

### Why 49.3 Is the Default, Not the Exception

The procedural record has become almost deterministic. Since the hung parliament produced by the 2024 dissolution, no French budget has been enacted through an ordinary majority vote — all have required the government to engage its responsibility under Article 49.3. [S28] Amendments approved by the Assembly have already widened the deficit by roughly €10 billion against the government's text, according to deputy David Amiel — a number that makes a voluntary withdrawal or a negotiated passage still less plausible, because the government cannot accept the fiscal arithmetic the floor has produced. [S11]

The mechanism matters. it converts a budget vote into a survival vote, and it does so on terrain where the government has repeatedly won. The counterintuitive implication is that the deeper the budget mess, the *more* likely enactment becomes — because the only actors who can kill the bill via censure are the same actors who have declined to do so five times running.

### The Twenty-Vote Arithmetic of Survival

Censure is the chokepoint of this scenario, and the arithmetic is unusually legible. only one censure motion has been adopted since 1958 (against Pompidou in 1962) [S39]. Lecornu has faced and survived five censure motions [S39].

Why does the gap hold? Because the two blocs capable of closing it have opposing incentives. The Rassemblement National's dominant strategy is to *wait*: bringing down Lecornu in late 2026 would either trigger a dissolution Macron has no reason to grant or force the RN to co-own an austerity budget months before an election it currently leads. [S40] The Socialists, meanwhile, abstain because censure without a path to govern would hand the initiative to RN and risk being blamed for fiscal chaos; the riots have intensified this caution, since the government has attempted to pin the 17 October violence on Mélenchon, an attribution the Paris prosecutor could not corroborate from arrestee profiles. [S65] A PS that votes to topple a government days after its own coalition was accused of stoking riots would be accepting a framing war on the government's chosen terrain.

The muddle-through wager is therefore precise: the PS abstention survives the budget endgame because the alternative is worse for both abstainers and voters-for-censure. This is also the scenario's principal falsification trigger. The same is true of a second rating agency moving to negative outlook after Moody's 23 October review of the Aa3/Negative rating and ahead of S&P's 27 November review: a coordinated sovereign downgrade would raise the political price of abstention and could push wavering PS deputies over the line. [S66]

### The Deficit Lands a Hair Below the Line

the IMF projects -5.352% [S8]. All sit below the threshold, with roughly 0.1 percentage points of headroom at the government's own number.

Muddle-through does not mean fiscal repair; it means slippage slow enough to stay under the line while the EDP clock keeps running.

### Order Holds Without Healing

The public-order pillar of this scenario is de-escalation without reconciliation. But within the muddle-through path, the operative fact is that the October riots were suppressed by targeted curfews in parts of Paris, Bordeaux, Lille and northern towns [S45][S47], credited by Lecornu with restoring calm, with a state of emergency held in reserve ("if needed") rather than invoked [S51].

Here the insured-loss economics provide the cleanest early-warning metric. In the muddle-through path, the first official France Assureurs tally lands below the 2023-episode asymptote in real scale, no catastrophe declaration follows, and the not-yet-operational state-backed riot-insurance mechanism created by the 2026 budget law staggers into force without a second stress test. [S5] The first post-riot poll showing voting intentions largely stable [S57] supports the political dimension of this pillar: riots, however costly, do not automatically move vote shares in either direction.

The response is that base rates are not destiny when the conditioning variable has changed: the October 2026 episode was triggered by a specific demonstration cycle, was met with pre-positioned curfew doctrine that did not exist in 2023, and occurred with six months of runway to the election — giving every mainstream actor an incentive to lower the temperature. [S64]

### An On-Time Election with Le Pen First, Under Strain

The final pillar is electoral continuity. The dates of 18 April and 2 May 2027 are constitutionally fixed and cannot be moved even under emergency regimes — the historical base rate of postponed elections under French emergency powers is zero for seven episodes, with the 2017 presidential rounds held under the November 2015 state of emergency. [S15] So the muddle-through election happens on time almost by construction; what the scenario adds is its content. the second qualification slot is contested among the left coalition, Édouard Philippe and the Macron-affiliated fragment — and that contest, not the first place, is where muddle-through's budget austerity does its electoral work, because a €54 billion squeeze [S31] administered by a surviving Lecornu government depresses incumbent-bloc vote share without offering the left a sufficient trigger to convert grievance into censure.

That is a narrow corridor, and the market pricing reflects its narrowness. A sustained move above the high-150s would signal that markets no longer believe the corridor holds — and would itself become a political variable, tightening the screws on exactly the PS deputies whose abstention the scenario requires.

### Resolution Criteria and Honest Falsification

Absent those triggers, the least glamorous outcome — a government that governs without a majority, a budget that passes without a vote, and an election that happens on time in a country on edge — remains the single best forecast available.

## Scenario Forecast II — Escalation and Paralysis (≈18%): Recurring Riots, Slipped Budget, Caretaker Cabinet

It is not a marginal extension of the muddle-through baseline; it is a distinct cascade in which public order, fiscal procedure and government survival feed on one another until France enters the April 2027 first round with a caretaker cabinet, no enacted 2027 budget and a sovereign spread pricing crisis-era levels. and a technocratic or caretaker cabinet in office as the campaign peaks. Each link in that chain is individually improbable in isolation; the scenario's internal logic is that each link raises the probability of the next.

### The Trigger: Why Riot Recurrence Is the Hinge Variable

The scenario begins with the one variable the government cannot control through procedural arithmetic. The historical base rate is sobering: of four prior severe French riot episodes since 2005, three were followed by another severe episode within five years, and the interval has been shrinking — roughly three years separated the 2023 Nahel riots from October 2026. [S17]

Two conditions would convert a recurrence into scenario-triggering escalation. That gap is the second-order fuse: insurers facing back-to-back catastrophe-scale riot losses without the reinsurance backstop may withdraw capacity or reprice, exactly as the FT-relayed framing of riots becoming "like natural catastrophes" suggests [S49].

Second, the emergency lever. Prime Minister Lecornu has already publicly pre-positioned it, saying a state of emergency could be declared "if needed," while crediting targeted overnight curfews in parts of Paris, Bordeaux, Lille and northern towns with restoring calm [S51][S45]. A second riot wave would make targeted curfews insufficient and force the formal declaration he has held in reserve. The critical point, developed below, is that this lever addresses order but not governance — and its use would itself become a campaign issue that amplifies the RN's security framing.

### The Cascade: From Streets to a Slipped Budget

The fiscal transmission runs through an already-frozen machine. Reuters's procedural analysis outlines what happens if nothing passes by year-end: an emergency "special law" (loi spéciale) rolling over the 2026 budget to permit tax collection, or the never-used ordinance "nuclear option," which legal experts expect would trigger censure if invoked without a passed budget.

Here is the scenario's core mechanism. Since the 2024 dissolution, every French budget has passed only through Article 49.3, and censure following a 49.3 engagement voids the bill. [S28] In the escalation path, a second riot episode does two things simultaneously: it makes the €54 billion austerity squeeze [S31] politically toxic for the Socialist Party to tolerate — cost-of-living grievances are precisely what the riots expressed — and it consumes the calendar, as emergency decrees, Assembly emergencies and curfew management crowd out budget negotiation in the decisive November-December window. If the PS abstention that has saved Lecornu five times out of five flips to censure, the bill dies with the government.

A slipped budget alone — without censure — is the more likely sub-variant within this scenario: the 17 November whole-budget vote fails, December collapses into a loi spéciale negotiation, and January 2027 opens with the state running on rolled-over 2026 appropriations. [S29]

The government-survival endgame follows. President Macron's options in a censure-voided-budget emergency are to dissolve (constitutionally exhausted as a practical matter this close to the presidential election), to appoint a technocrat, or to govern by emergency powers [background]. A technocratic caretaker cabinet managing a loi spéciale through the EDP timeline — France among nine member states still under the Excessive Deficit Procedure — into an election it cannot contest is the scenario's terminal political state: paralysis by design, with no actor having an incentive to resolve it before April.

The distinction this report draws — developed further in Chapter 9 — is between riot-driven volatility, which is transitory, and fiscal-slippage repricing, which is persistent. Escalation converts the transitory into the persistent: a second riot episode plus a slipped budget plus a caretaker cabinet is precisely the combination that reprices sovereign credit rather than risk appetite. French 10-year yields above Greek yields already reflect market pricing of French fiscal fragility [insight retrieval]; [S11]

So the scenario does not predict postponement; it predicts an election held on schedule (18 April / 2 May 2027, dates the Constitutional Council guards and that no emergency regime can move) under deteriorating conditions that structurally favour the front-runner. ## Scenario Forecast III — Censure and Left Surge (≈12%): The 20-Vote Gap Closes

The scenario is not fantastical — it is arithmetically within twenty votes of reality — but it requires a party currently practising strategic restraint to abandon that restraint at precisely the moment when abandoning it serves the presidential ambitions of its rivals. That tension is why the probability stays low despite the smallest parliamentary distance of any scenario in this report.

### The Arithmetic: Twenty Votes and One Precedent Since 1958

abstentions and absences count as support for the government. The historical record is a warning against overpricing this path. Only one censure motion has been adopted since 1958 — against Georges Pompidou's government in 1962 over de Gaulle's direct-election referendum — out of dozens tabled. [S39] Sébastien Lecornu himself has faced and survived five censure motions, a perfect survival record [S39][S40].

That twenty-vote gap is the entire scenario. Two blocs could in principle close it. Opportunism points RN toward abstention unless the government's fall can be attributed entirely to the left — and even then, joining the censure vote costs it the ability to run against "the censure coalition."

This leaves the PS as the single hinge. And the hinge is rusty for reasons specific to the 2027 calendar: a PS that votes censure alongside Mélenchon's La France Insoumise strengthens exactly the competitor it must beat — or at least co-exist with — in the left's presidential primary dynamics.

### What Could Shift the PS: The €54bn Squeeze and Riot-Amplified Grievances

The trigger inventory for a PS shift has three plausible entries. If the final text leans visibly on social spending, health insurance or local government transfers rather than on tax measures acceptable to the left, the PS's abstention becomes politically expensive inside its own base. A text that swings right in the endgame to placate Les Républicains is the classic configuration that historically moves social-democratic deputies from abstention to censure.

The second trigger is the riots. The government's response has been order-heavy: Lecornu credited the curfews with restoring calm and said a state of emergency could be declared "if needed" [S51], while Macron urged calm without invoking emergency powers [S15]. For the left, this posture converts a cost-of-living protest wave into a policing story, and the government's attempt to blame Mélenchon for stoking the 17 October violence has not been corroborated by arrestee profiles, according to Paris prosecutor Laure Beccuau. [S65] Crucially, the first post-riot poll showed presidential voting intentions largely stable [S57] — Mélenchon's numbers did not move. Sustained repression plus austerity is the compound most likely to force the PS's hand.

The third trigger is procedural humiliation. A 49.3 engagement on the whole budget would be the PS's last clear decision point: abstain and let the bill pass, or vote censure and void it — because censure after a 49.3 engagement voids the bill itself. [S28] Reuters's explainer further notes that if nothing passes, legal experts expect the never-used ordinance "nuclear option" or an emergency special law rolling over the 2026 budget, and that the ordinance route would itself be expected to trigger censure.

### Steelmanning the Low Probability — and Why the Case Still Fails

The strongest version of the censure case deserves articulation. Markets have already repriced: the 10-year OAT-Bund spread hit a one-year high of 150.8 bps on 20 October, with French yields above Italy's and Greece's [S12]. If a rating agency downgrades — Moody's reviews Aa3/Negative on 23 October, S&P reviews A+/Stable on 27 November — the government's competence narrative collapses, and censure becomes a "responsibility" vote rather than an opportunistic one. [S66]

This report's response is that the mechanism is real but the actor alignment is absent. Second, the riot salience paradox: security framing amplifies RN, not the left — the OpinionWay and Odoxa polls showing Le Pen rising across scenarios in October [S17] suggest that even a second riot wave pushes the electorate toward the party of order, making a left surge electorally incoherent even if censeure succeeded governmentally.

### Resolution Criteria, Falsification and Early Signposts

Falsification arrives through two channels.

The dated watchlist for updating this scenario's probability: any PS statement on censure intentions before mid-November; and a second riot episode severe enough to force fresh curfews, which — given the 3-in-4 recurrence base rate — is the single external event most capable of converting the PS's cold electoral calculus into hot parliamentary arithmetic. Absent those signals, the twenty-vote gap remains what it has been throughout this legislature: visible, measurable, and politically uncrossable.

## Public Order Forecast: Riot Recurrence, Insurance Losses and Emergency Powers

The October 2026 riots are the single most consequential non-fiscal variable in France's path to the April 2027 ballot. [S66] This chapter forecasts the public-order trajectory across three questions: whether large-scale rioting recurs before the first round on 18 April 2027; [S15] how the insured-loss picture will evolve as claims mature; and whether the state will resort to emergency powers — and what that means for the election itself.

### The anatomy of October 2026: what the damage data already tells us

The baseline facts are sobering. This dispute matters beyond morbidity accounting: contested death tolls are precisely the raw material of radicalisation narratives on both extremes, and any confirmation of additional deaths would raise the emotional temperature of future mobilisations.

Crucially, no insured-loss figure yet exists.

### Why recurrence is the base-rate expectation — and the steelman against it

The steelman against recurrence deserves serious weight. The October de-escalation was fast and effective: Lecornu explicitly credited the targeted curfews — imposed in parts of Paris, Bordeaux, Lille and northern towns — with restoring calm [S45][S47], promised a "massive" police presence on subsequent weekends [S13], and kept a state of emergency in reserve, saying one could be declared "if needed" [S51]. Macron's refusal to take the emergency-state bait on 18 October [S15] preserved a proportionality margin that a full emergency declaration would have exhausted. And the first post-riot poll showed presidential voting intentions largely stable [S57] — evidence that the political market, at least initially, did not price a revolutionary dynamic. On this reading, October was a contained shock, and the electoral calendar itself (discussed below) gives every mainstream actor an incentive to keep the streets quiet through April.

My reconciliation: the de-escalation machinery works against spontaneous recurrence but not against triggered recurrence. I discount that deliberation's overall weighting — it under-weighted the demonstrated effectiveness of the October response template and RN's rational preference for a calm run-in to an election it is poised to win — but its risk-window identification is sound.

### The insurance gap: a mechanism created but not yet battle-tested

Morningstar's analysis framed the October events precisely as a test arriving before the new mechanism is in place [S48]. The near-term consequence is that the October losses fall on standard property and business-interruption policies — absorbable for major insurers below the EUR 1.2 billion ceiling, but painful for the mid-market and for specialty retail carriers. Three second-order risks follow.

The rational market response is premium hardening or coverage withdrawal in high-risk commercial districts — which would push uninsured riot exposure onto the state precisely as the mechanism meant to socialise it is still in bureaucratic gestation.

Second, the catastrophe-declaration question. Riot damage is normally covered without a natural-catastrophe declaration, but a ministerial catastrophe order would activate the CCR's state-guaranteed reinsurance channel and shift a portion of the tail onto the public balance sheet. Watch France Assureurs' first official estimate and any prefectoral or ministerial declaration as the two cleanest observable signals — both are on the Chapter 10 watchlist.

This gap is itself a mild accelerant of the escalation scenario's market leg.

### Emergency powers: 2017 is the precedent, postponement is not on the table

The legal architecture sharply constrains the darkest readings. The correct forecasting frame is therefore not "will the election be postponed? " — effectively a nil-probability tail — but "under what security regime will it be held? "

Three regimes are plausible. The Constitutional Council, arbiter of both election rules and emergency-measure legality, would face litigation over any emergency measures touching campaign gatherings or polling-day organisation; the council's likely posture, consistent with its historical role, is to permit security measures that protect the franchise while striking measures that burden it. The third regime — targeted curfews recurring in specific cities during the campaign without a national emergency — is the intermediate case and the most probable form any pre-election restriction would take, given that the October curfews were city-district-scoped precisely to preserve proportionality [S45][S47].

One counterintuitive electoral mechanism deserves emphasis. It is often assumed that unrest depresses turnout; the French record suggests the opposite for salience-raising security episodes — order-and-security framing mobilises RN's base and pushes undecided voters toward the party of order, while cost-of-living grievances mobilise the left. The first post-riot poll's stability [S57] shows these forces largely cancelling in the aggregate, which means the riots' principal electoral effect is not a level shift but a composition shift: higher salience of security (RN-favourable) and purchasing power (left-favourable) at the expense of the centre's competence-and-stability pitch — a dynamic Chapter 8 quantifies.

The survival of the Lecornu government through the first presidential round on 18 April 2027 is, in this report's judgment, the single most over-analyzed variable in French politics today — and the one most likely to resolve in favour of the status quo. [S64]

### The Censure Ledger: Why 269 Is the Number That Matters

The historical record calibrates the odds. For the government to fall, three things must simultaneously happen: the NFP must turn out its full bloc, the PS must abandon its strategic abstention, and either LR deputies or a slice of the centre must defect. Each condition is individually plausible; jointly they have not occurred once under this configuration.

The steelman against this reading is that the October riots change the calculus. Cost-of-living grievances are the PS's competitive turf against Mélenchon, and voting 'present' while a right-wing minority government imposes austerity after deadly unrest is exactly the position that hemorrhages left votes to LFI. But two offsetting forces argue against closure of the gap. First, the government's attempt to blame Mélenchon for stoking the 17 October violence has left his voting intentions unmoved, and Paris prosecutor Laure Beccuau could not corroborate the attribution from arrestee profiles — meaning the riot has not (yet) produced a clean political winner whose momentum drags the PS. The Rassemblement National's calculus is the least appreciated stabiliser of the current government. LR's pivotal role deserves quantification. The credible threat of RN–NFP convergence is LR's leverage; the government's survival is LR's price.

## Election Forecast: First-Round Qualification, Runoff Odds and How the Riots Shift Votes

The electoral question running through France's crisis is whether the October 2026 riots and the budget war reshape the 2027 presidential race before the first round on 18 April 2027. [S64] The riots, contrary to the government's initial framing, have so far moved almost nothing in the polls, and the mechanism by which they *could* move votes runs through turnout and second-place fragmentation rather than through headline vote-share shifts.

### The Polling Spread: Wide but Directionally Unambiguous

Two readings of this spread matter for forecasting. First, the dispersion is driven mainly by scenario composition — which candidates actually run — rather than sampling error. An Elabe field that includes a stronger centre consolidation mechanically compresses Le Pen's score; an Odoxa field with a fractured centre inflates it. This means the spread is not noise to be averaged away: it is itself information about the second-place battle. Second, and more striking, the *direction* was already upward before the riots: OpinionWay's mid-October poll showed Le Pen up from September in all scenarios [S17], and Reuters reported in mid-October that both Le Pen and Mélenchon were gaining momentum [S1].

The most important single data point for the riot question is the first post-riot poll: it showed French presidential voting intentions largely unaffected by the unrest [S57]. They have not yet done so.

### The Blame Mechanism That Failed

Two things went wrong with that attribution. Mélenchon's voting intentions did not move [S57]. And, more damaging to the government's credibility, Paris prosecutor Laure Beccuau could not confirm the government's account from arrestee profiles. When the judicial evidence does not corroborate a political narrative, the narrative loses its second-week legs — and the polling stability is consistent with voters discounting it.

This matters structurally. The scenario in which security salience lifts RN *at the expense of the left* requires the left to be plausibly linked to the disorder. The prosecutorial record so far does not support that link. What security salience can still do is lift the salience of order-and-authority themes generally — which benefits RN on the second-order channel of turnout and agenda-setting, not on direct vote transfers from Mélenchon. Mélenchon's own trajectory, tracked since September as on course for a face-off against Le Pen in POLITICO's reading of the polls [S54], remains intact.

### Second Place: The Real Contest

While first place is close to settled, second place is where the election's coalition logic will be decided. Three blocs compete:

- **The left (NFP/Mélenchon).** Momentum reported since early autumn, and riot-related blame that failed to stick.
- The polling spread's sensitivity to scenario composition is largely a sensitivity to how this three-way resolves.
- **RN beyond Le Pen.** OpinionWay's data showed Le Pen rising even as Jordan Bardella's numbers fell in one September snapshot [S55], suggesting the party's presidential brand is more concentrated than its bench — a fragility if Le Pen's eligibility were again contested, though nothing in the current record makes that the modal path.

The forecast here: Mélenchon is more likely than any single centre-right figure to finish second, because the left enters with one candidate and the moderate bloc enters with at least two credible ones.

### Runoff Odds and the 0-for-2 Base Rate

That 0-for-2 record is the correct external anchor for any runoff probability, but it must be adjusted for one large change: she has never before entered a runoff from a first-round score in the mid-30s.

Even so, the runoff arithmetic remains demanding. This report therefore treats Le Pen as a genuine runoff contender with the runoff itself closer than in either prior attempt — but not as the favourite. This is consistent with the report's modal muddle-through path, in which the election proceeds on schedule with RN qualifying first.

### Turnout: The Underpriced Channel

The riots interact with the electorate through two opposing turnout mechanisms. Security salience — looted shops, curfews in Paris, Bordeaux and Lille [S45], a "massive" police posture [S13] — tends to mobilise older, order-prioritising voters who lean right and vote reliably anyway.

The net effect of October's events is therefore ambiguous and probably small: the mobilising grievance for the left (austerity) and for the right (disorder) were both refreshed in the same fortnight. The first post-riot poll's stability [S57] is consistent with exactly this offset.

### Emergency Powers and the Election Calendar

One electoral question can be answered with high confidence now: the election will not be postponed. The Constitutional Council's arbiter role over election rules reinforces this.

### Against the Market Consensus — and With It Where It Counts

Polymarket's snapshot of the 2027 French presidential market prices long-shot candidates such as Ségolène Royal and Olivier Faure at effectively 0% [Polymarket], and the market volume on the event is substantial (~$7m per candidate line). The zero pricing of second-tier establishment figures is directionally consistent with this chapter's fragmentation analysis: the moderate bloc's problem is not that it lacks votes but that it lacks a single claimant the market will price. Where this report goes further than the market snapshot: the market's pricing of the left's line deserves scrutiny, because Mélenchon's stable post-riot numbers and the failed blame attribution suggest his second-place probability is not being discounted by riot contagion — the risk traders might otherwise have priced in.

**Bottom line:** Le Pen first with high confidence; second place contested with Mélenchon the most likely single qualifier against a split moderate field; the riots so far neutral in headline polls but potent through turnout composition if they recur; runoff closer than ever before yet still shaped by Le Pen's 0-for-2 record; and the election on schedule on 18 April 2027, whatever the state of emergency. Forecasting is only as good as its falsification criteria. ### The Contrarian Case: Why Stabilisation Is Not Impossible (and Why It Still Loses)

Steelman first. The PS has abstained on censure rather than vote with RN, showing it fears election-triggering instability. LR has every reason to demonstrate governing responsibility ahead of a presidential cycle in which its voters' second-choice preferences matter.

Because every incentive points the right way and every precedent points the wrong way. and the solemn first-part vote was postponed — none of which is the texture of a chamber converging on consensus. The €10bn of deficit-widening amendments means a cross-party deal would have to *remove* spending the Assembly just added, in public, weeks before a presidential campaign.

### Decision Implications

a retreat through the 120s confirms muddle-through. CAC hedging is asymmetrically cheap against the escalation branch;

Reinsurers should treat the below-€1.2bn Morningstar cap as conditional on no second event and price retro cover accordingly.

softening it invites the spread widening it fears.

**For party strategists.** For the PS, the abstention-vs-censure margin is the single highest-leverage decision of the cycle: abstention preserves the muddle-through baseline; For the Macron camp, the data argue against dissolution (a tail this report prices near zero) and for exactly what the record shows: endure censure votes, pass the budget by 49.3, and let the election proceed on schedule — as it did in 2017, under a state of emergency if necessary. ### Calibration I: The Documented Base Rates and How They Were Combined

The probabilities in the table are not opinions; they are adjustments to documented frequencies. Five base rates do nearly all the work.

A naive outside view would price government survival near-certain on this alone.

**Riot recurrence: 3 of 4 severe episodes followed by another within five years, at a shortening interval.** This is the base rate that pushes hardest against the modal path.

This is the one base rate applied almost without adjustment: postponement is priced as a nil tail, and the correct question is the security regime under which an on-time election is held.

Two triangulated quantities deserve separate note because their calibration rests on conflicting published estimates rather than frequencies. Where prediction markets overlap, they are directionally consistent: Polymarket prices second-tier establishment figures such as Royal and Faure at effectively 0%, corroborating the fragmentation analysis of the moderate bloc.

### Calibration II: Where the Internal Deliberation Disagreed — and Why This Report Published Lower

The divergence is concentrated in one variable: riot recurrence.

This report publishes the lower figure for three documented reasons, and the disagreement is instructive rather than embarrassing.

First, the deliberative weighting under-weighted the demonstrated effectiveness of the October containment. The targeted curfews in parts of Paris, Bordeaux, Lille and northern towns were credited by Lecornu himself with restoring calm [S45][S47]; a state of emergency was explicitly held in reserve rather than invoked [S51]; and the first post-riot poll showed presidential voting intentions largely stable [S57].

Second, the deliberative path under-weighted the strategic patience of the actors whose joint behaviour escalation requires. Escalation needs the RN to abandon patience or the PS to abandon abstention, and the deliberative weighting implicitly assumed one of these flips was more likely than the observed record supports.

Third, and most structurally, the deliberative exercise treated the causal chain — riots to censure to budget failure to spread blowout — as more tightly coupled than the evidence warrants. The chain's links are real (Chapter 10 maps them), but each link has a demonstrated escape valve: the 2017 precedent decouples emergency rule from election delay; [S15] the loi spéciale decovers budget failure from state shutdown; Coupling exists, but it is partial, and compounding partial couplings through five links compounds the discount too.

Where the deliberation *did* move the published numbers is in the residual bin. The deliberative exercise flagged, repeatedly, that partial combinations — a passed budget followed by riot recurrence, a survived censure followed by deficit slippage — were more probable than any clean scenario narrative admitted.

### Calibration III: Evidence Gaps and Their Asymmetric Consequences

Four gaps in the evidence base materially shape the confidence intervals, and each cuts in a different direction.

**No official insured-loss figure exists.** France Assureurs stated on 20 October that it could not yet provide an estimate; [S5] third-party figures span roughly €700m to below €1.2bn, with Morningstar's cap conditional on no further riots.

**The death toll is contested.** The Interior Ministry's single, unconfirmed death sits against CNN's tally of at least two. Contested death tolls are raw material for radicalisation narratives on both extremes; confirmation of additional deaths is a qualitative escalator the numbers cannot capture.

### The Residual Bin at 30 April 2027: An Interpretation Key

Because the residual bin is the second-largest number in the table, its interpretation at classification time matters as much as its size. The bin is not a garbage category; it is the report's structural claim that France's most probable *single* outcome is a named scenario, but its most probable *texture* is a mixture. Three archetypes will dominate it.

This archetype inherits muddle-through's politics (Le Pen first, on-time election) with escalation's fiscal arithmetic, and is the single most likely occupant of the bin. The second is **escalation without full paralysis**: a second riot episode and a slipped budget, but Lecornu or a same-bloc successor — not a technocrat — still in office on polling day, France having already demonstrated with the 2 February 2026 adoption of the budget that it can operate a month into the year on rollover mechanisms. [S27]

Check the five joint criteria of Scenario 1 against the record: Journal Officiel entry and instrument; the first-round result; Then Scenario 2's four, then Scenario 3's three. If any one criterion of a named scenario fails, the outcome is residual — even if four of five pillars held.

### The Final Ledger

One paragraph of synthesis. The probabilities rest on five documented frequencies and three triangulated quantities; The forecast is calibrated, checkable, and most probably about to be confirmed in its broadest stroke and falsified in one detail. That is what a medium-confidence forecast in a compound crisis should look like.
